## 0. How to read this document

### 0.1 Status tags

Every normative statement carries exactly one tag so that decisions, proposals and unknowns cannot be confused.

| Tag | Meaning | May implementation rely on it? |
|---|---|---|
| **[C]** Confirmed | Taken from a decision the owner made in the discovery interview (`REQUIREMENTS.md`, or the "Explicitly decided" list in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`). Cites its source. | Yes. |
| **[P]** Proposed | A design choice or safe default written by the spec author to make [C] items implementable. Reversible unless §24 says otherwise. | Yes where its §24 row is **Decided**. Otherwise it may guide prototypes and development, but needs a separate owner decision before production use (§25.6). |
| **[OQ-nn]** Unresolved | Depends on an owner decision or an artifact not yet supplied. Shown with the safe interim behavior. | No. Implement only the stated safe default, and only where §24 permits it. |

**Register overrides tags.** Where a §24 register row reads **Decided**, the safe default described for it in the body (even if still tagged **[P]**) is owner-approved. Body tags are not individually rewritten; the register is authoritative for decided rows.

"Requirement" language: **must** = mandatory, **should** = strong default, **may** = optional.

### 0.2 Identifiers

Requirements carry stable IDs of the form `AREA-n` (e.g. `CHK-4`). IDs are never reused or renumbered; retired requirements are marked *Withdrawn*. Acceptance criteria use `AC-n` (§23). Decision-register entries use `OQ-nn` (§24); OQ-01–OQ-27 come from `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, and OQ-28 onward were raised while writing this spec.

Source citations such as `(REQ §2.3)` point at sections of `REQUIREMENTS.md`; `(OQ list: decided)` points at the "Explicitly decided" list in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`; `(HO inv. 5)` points at design invariant 5 in `PROJECT_HANDOFF.md`.

## 1. Purpose, scope and non-goals

**1.1 Purpose [C]** (REQ §1). A mobile-friendly, responsive web application for an EMS organization to inspect ambulances and movable equipment, identify and resolve deficiencies, track operational readiness, manage supplies and assets, and keep audit-ready maintenance and regulatory records.

**1.2 Tenancy [C]** (REQ §1). The first release serves one organization. **[P]** The data model carries an `organization_id` on organization-owned entities so multi-organization support is not precluded, but v1 provides no cross-organization administration, billing or isolation features.

**1.3 Non-goals [C]** (HO "Product vision"). The system is not a crew timeclock, ambulance billing platform, patient chart (ePCR), or dispatch/CAD replacement. It must not store patient-identifying data (see §16.5).

**1.4 Core principles [C]** (REQ §1; HO invariants). These govern every other section; where a later rule appears to conflict, the principle wins and the conflict is a spec defect to be fixed by amendment.

| ID | Principle |
|---|---|
| PRN-1 | A specific **item check**, not a crew or whole-vehicle session, satisfies an inspection obligation. |
| PRN-2 | A crew member may inspect items on multiple vehicles; multiple crew members may work on one vehicle concurrently. |
| PRN-3 | Pass, exemption/suspension, Unable to Inspect, and unresolved deficiency are distinct states and are never conflated. |
| PRN-4 | History is immutable and attributed: inspections, corrections, verifications, cancellations and administrative invalidations. |
| PRN-5 | Alerts go to humans; defect severity and regulatory overdue status never change ambulance operational status automatically. |
| PRN-6 | A recent Pass never erases an unresolved genuine failure. |
| PRN-7 | The product is online-only; pending and failed saves are always visible. |

## 3. Roles and authorization

**3.1 Built-in roles [C]** (REQ §7): **Crew**, **Supervisor**, **Supply Officer**, **Administrator**. A user may hold several. A newly provisioned valid user receives **Crew** only; elevated roles are assigned by an administrator, and every grant/revocation is audit-logged. Crew members performing checks may hold various clinical certifications (e.g. EMT, EMR, Paramedic, Driver) or none (e.g. probationary members/trainees), and may be volunteer agency members ("members") or paid employees ("employees"); all authorized personnel performing inspections share the baseline Crew role. Inspection records capture individual attribution (actor, instant, session) without requiring clinical titles; proof of clinical qualification and driver status is established by cross-referencing external roster (Humanity) and organization personnel/training records.

**3.2 Authorization model [P].** Authorization is enforced **server-side on every request**, deny-by-default, using the union of the actor's roles. A UI control being hidden is never the enforcement mechanism. Role grants are evaluated at request time (not cached beyond the session in a way that outlives revocation).

## 4. Fleet, equipment and checklist model

### 4.1 Fleet [C] (REQ §2.2)

Vehicles are identified by a stable internal ID and a display name. The demonstration fleet shows four ambulance units (Ambulance 1 through Ambulance 4); names, numbers and VINs are synthetic until supplied, and the application does not limit an organization to four vehicles. Each vehicle has an operational status (§9), an optional "Out of service" routine-check exemption period (§5.9), an odometer, and optional engine hours (§6.8).

### 4.2 Location hierarchy [C] (REQ §2.1, §3)

Checklist items may belong to **compartments**, **kits**, **equipment**, or **ambulance-wide systems**. **[P]** Logical hierarchy:

```
Vehicle
 ├─ Compartment (e.g. "Driver-side exterior compartment 1")
 │   ├─ Kit/Container instance (physical, §10)     ← may move between vehicles/stations
 │   │   └─ Item
 │   └─ Item
 ├─ Equipment asset instance (serialized, §11)     ← may move between vehicles
 └─ Vehicle system (e.g. "Lights and sirens") ─ Item
```

Kits and tracked equipment are physical objects that can change vehicle assignment. A checklist item therefore binds to a **location slot** (vehicle compartment/system) or to a **kit/asset type**; which one is OQ-01 (it depends on the real checklists). **[P]** Safe default for equipment assets and for items outside a kit: bind to the slot for R1 and record the asset identity that was present at check time on each check record, so movement (R2) never rewrites history. **[C: owner-decided 2026-10-24, OQ-53]** A **kit is a template** (a kit type), and a vehicle keeps **zero, one or several instances** of it. Every instance needs its own checks (quantities, expirations and the rest), so an item inside a kit yields one obligation **per kit instance** (§5.1): checking one instance never satisfies the requirement for another, and a kit that is not on the vehicle is not required. Whether a vehicle has the right number of a kit in the right place is checked against its own kit list (§4.6 KDEF-9, KDEF-10; amendment 2.0.0). A check names the kit instance it covers (§7.2). A kit type is recorded as a versioned kit definition (§4.6), and each kit instance names its definition.

### 4.3 Vehicle checklists and versions [C] (REQ §2.1; amendment 5.0.0, [issue #220](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/220))

- **CHK-1 [C] Vehicle-owned checklists.** Each vehicle owns one or more complete vehicle-specific checklists. Inactive vehicles retain their own checklists under CFG-4. Each checklist defines that vehicle's non-kit items to inspect and each item's check properties and optional placement in one of that vehicle's compartments or systems. It may name shared kit definitions to inspect; kit contents and physical kit instances continue to follow §4.6. No central checklist template, template binding or overlay supplies items or placements to more than one vehicle. Editing one vehicle checklist does not change another vehicle's checklist, even when both were copied from the same export.
- **CHK-2 [C] Vehicle checklist versions and schedule assignment.** A vehicle checklist is drafted, edited and published as immutable versions. Each checklist belongs to one vehicle; schedule assignment is a separate many-to-many relationship. One checklist may serve multiple shared schedule slots, and a vehicle may have multiple checklists assigned to the same slot. Each assignment has its own effective interval. Publication and effective-time rules in CHK-3 apply. An edit creates a new version; it never changes a published version or a session already pinned to it. Do not merge checklists assigned to the same slot or duplicate a checklist solely because it serves another slot.
- **CHK-3** Publishing is explicit and may specify a future **effective date-time**. The effective instant determines which obligations the version governs (§5.7). For a due instant `D`, each vehicle checklist version assigned to the relevant shared schedule slot and effective at `D` governs; each eligible assigned checklist contributes its own items and obligations, and checklist identities are never merged (amendment 5.0.0).
- **CHK-4** An inspection session records and retains its original checklist version (the vehicle checklist version, amendment 5.0.0). Reopening never replaces it with newer contents.
- **CHK-5 [C] Checklist version contents.** A published vehicle checklist version retains the vehicle item requirements it inspects (CHK-11), the kit definitions it names, their display order and its schedule rules. Each item requirement's place and its other requirements (instructions, expected quantity, kind, bulk-pass eligibility, allowed outcomes, conditional rules and Not-Applicable rules) come from that requirement's version in force at the obligation's due instant (amendment 6.0.0). A version published before amendment 6.0.0 keeps the placement and requirements it recorded, for history and for the obligations that pin it (§18.3). Kit definitions included for inspection remain shared references; their kit items and instances follow KDEF-1–KDEF-12. A null placement means **Not placed**; it does not remove the item from the checklist, its due obligations or its history.
- **CHK-6 [Withdrawn by amendment 5.0.0].** The former vehicle-override overlay on a centrally managed template is retired. The checklist for a vehicle is its own complete published configuration.
- **CHK-7 [OQ-01]** The real items, quantities, locations, instructions, intervals and exceptions have not been supplied. Seed data in any environment must be synthetic and labeled non-authoritative. The spec defines the model only; it asserts no stocking requirement.
- **CHK-8 [C] No shared checklist placement.** A vehicle checklist's membership and non-kit item placement apply only to that vehicle. Position identity is vehicle-local. Equal labels or file-local identifiers on separate vehicles do not connect their checklists. Shared schedule slots and kit definitions remain shared as specified in §4.6 and §5.
- **CHK-9 [C] Unplaced items remain due.** A checklist item whose placement is null remains in the applicable checklist and continues to generate and satisfy obligations under the ordinary schedule, kind, window and outcome rules. The inspection presents it in a clearly named **Not placed** group, not under a compartment/system and not as exempt, optional or satisfied. A Supervisor places it by publishing a new vehicle checklist version that points to a valid position on that vehicle. The placement change does not rewrite earlier versions, obligations, checks or history.
- **CHK-10 [C] Deterministic routine-anchor precedence.** Items remain organization-wide records referenced by vehicle-checklist versions. When multiple assigned checklist versions require the same `(vehicle, item requirement, kit instance, due instant D)` routine anchor (amendment 6.0.0; before it, `(vehicle, item, kit instance, D)`), resolve one requirement using the implemented precedence: lowest schedule-slot text value under code-point ordering, then lowest legacy checklist identity (`template_id`) for migrated checklists. Persist this as an explicit stable precedence ordinal for each vehicle checklist; new checklists receive ordinals after existing ones. The selected requirement supplies the anchor's effective requirement version; do not create competing routine obligations. Preserve the ordinal across publication, vehicle duplication and export/import; migration derives it from the current generator's identity ordering. This codifies current implemented behavior that was previously unspecified and does not refuse checklist publication, assignment, import, schedule-rule changes or kit movement. Linking of one check to obligations with distinct due instants follows SCH-6.
- **CHK-11 [C: owner decisions 2026-11-21, amendment 6.0.0] One requirement per item and place on a vehicle.** A vehicle item requirement is a stable record of one stock of an item on one vehicle. That stock is kept in one place at a time: a compartment or system of that vehicle, or Not placed (CHK-9). The place belongs to each version, not to the requirement's identity. Its versions record the place, kind, expected quantity and unit, allowed outcomes, bulk-pass eligibility, Not-Applicable rules, conditional rules and instructions; they are effective-dated and published under CHK-3 by the roles that publish that vehicle's checklists (§3.3). An item kept in several places on a vehicle at once has one requirement for each place. At any instant, a vehicle has at most one requirement in force for one item in one place. Moving an item publishes a new version of its requirement; adding a place creates another requirement. **[C: owner decision 2026-11-21]** A move keeps the requirement's identity: it is the same stock in a new place. Its obligations, including one whose window is open when the move takes effect, are satisfied by a check at its current place, and each obligation and check keeps the place of the version it recorded, for history. Every checklist on the vehicle that inspects that item in that place uses the same requirement, so two checklists can never require different things of it. Item records remain organization-wide. Kit contents keep their kit definition's requirements and their kit instance's place, and kit presence its kit-list line (KDEF-1 to KDEF-12). A position cannot be retired while an item requirement in force or published for a later instant uses it (CFG-2).

### 4.4 Item kinds [P]

To make bulk-pass eligibility (§7.6) and verification scoping (§8.7) decidable, every item definition has a **kind**:

| Kind | Examples (synthetic) | Result entry |
|---|---|---|
| Presence/condition | "Suction unit present and clean" | Pass / Fail / Unable / NA |
| Quantity | "Oxygen masks: 6" | Observed count + Pass/Fail derived against exact target (§10.4) |
| Measurement | "Cylinder pressure ≥ X" | Recorded value; outcome derived or confirmed |
| Special observation | Items requiring seal/expiry inspection | As defined by item |
| Vehicle reading | Odometer | Value (§6.8) |

Quantity, Measurement and Special-observation items are **never bulk-pass eligible** (relevant only if Bulk Pass is restored; deferred from R1, §7.6) (REQ §2.3: "Do not bulk-pass items requiring direct measurements or special observations").

### 4.5 Temporary stocking deviations [C] (REQ §2.1)

Authorized, vehicle-specific, time-bounded deviations record: normal quantity, approved alternate quantity, validity start/end, approver, explanation, and full history. The system alerts before expiry. Extension is an audited new revision. A crew member may request early termination; a request requires approval (approver role OQ-06). While valid, the alternate quantity replaces the normal target for quantity checks on that vehicle; outside validity the normal target applies.

### 4.6 Kit definitions [C] (owner decisions 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-13, [issue #129](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/129))

- **KDEF-1** A **kit definition** is the kit type of §4.2 (OQ-53) made explicit: an organization-scoped record of what a complete kit contains. Each content entry names an item and its kind (§4.4), expected quantity, unit, instructions, allowed outcomes, display order and whether its expiration is tracked. The contents are defined once per definition and are never copied per vehicle or per kit instance.
- **KDEF-2** Kit definitions are versioned like vehicle checklists (CHK-2, CHK-3): a version is drafted and edited, is immutable once published, and is published explicitly with an effective instant that is never earlier than the publication. Every create, edit, duplicate, publish and deactivate is an audited command. Duplicating creates a new definition from the contents of a chosen version, with none of the original's history. A deactivated definition cannot be given to a new kit instance; instances that already have it keep it.
- **KDEF-3** Every kit instance names exactly one kit definition, fixed when the instance is created. A physical kit that changes type is recorded as the old instance deactivated and a new instance added (TIME-1a), so a recorded check, seal event or obligation never changes meaning.
- **KDEF-4** A checklist version names the kit definitions it inspects, so their presence and contents are checked on that checklist's schedule; it names no vehicle compartment or expected count. For a due instant `D` of a checklist that inspects definition X, each physical kit instance with definition X that assignment history says is assigned to the vehicle at `D` owes one presence obligation and one obligation per content entry of the version of X effective at `D`. Each presence and content obligation pins the instance and its uninterrupted assignment event. A definition version published later does not change obligations already created and in window, as for checklist versions (§5.7). A physical unit in unassigned stock creates no vehicle expectation. Checklist kit placements select which kit definitions are inspected; assignments determine which physical units the vehicle is expected to carry.
- **KDEF-5** A kit definition contains items only; it cannot contain another kit. A container inside a kit is modelled as its own kit definition and kit instance (OQ-67).
- **KDEF-6** A kit's contents are checked per assigned physical instance. The separate presence item reports whether that assigned unit is present; manually maintained vehicle kit counts are not a second source of expected inventory. An unassigned unit creates neither a presence obligation nor content obligations on a vehicle.
- **KDEF-7** Item identity is unchanged: a check, correction, deficiency, verification scope or seal event still names the item and the kit instance it covers (§7.2, SEAL-10). A sealed kit's listed contents (SEAL-1) are the contents of its definition version effective at the relevant instant.
- **KDEF-8** Configuration that exists before kit definitions is not rewritten. Published versions, obligations, checks and history keep their meaning. Kit contents move to definitions only through a new publication, which takes effect at its effective instant like any other (§5.7).
- **KDEF-9** A vehicle's expected physical kits are the kit instances whose append-only assignment history places them on that vehicle at the due instant. A kit list is retained as historical evidence for older inspections, but current administration does not create or edit independent required-count lines. Assignment to a vehicle and compartment takes effect when recorded and cannot be backdated. Moving or returning a kit ends the prior assignment run; a later assignment starts a new run. The assignment event history, not a cached count, determines expectations.
- **KDEF-10** For a due instant `D` of a checklist that inspects definition X (KDEF-4), each assigned physical instance of X owes one kit-presence obligation. The inspector records a Quantity check of the presence item against target 1 for that specific instance. A present unit passes with count 1; a missing unit fails with count 0 and opens a deficiency (§6) tied to the physical kit. The presence row is shown under the human-readable physical-kit label and is keyed by the unit, not a vehicle kit-list line. A unit assigned after its inspection window opened waits for the next due instant (TIME-1a).
- **KDEF-11** A kit is ready only when its presence obligation and the content obligations of its assigned instance are satisfied. An assigned but missing unit fails its own presence check, so the vehicle is not ready. Content checks remain tied to the physical unit and its assignment history; returning or moving a unit retires future obligations without rewriting past inspection results.
- **KDEF-12** Each kit definition has a **presence item**: an organization item created with the definition and named after it, used only for kit-presence checks and never as kit content. A new physical-kit presence obligation is an ordinary obligation of that item outside kit contents (§5.1), but it pins the assigned kit instance, its current assignment-run event, and the checklist placement. Its stream key is the physical kit id, so two otherwise identical units have separate obligations. Its check is a Quantity check against target 1, names the physical `kit_id`, and carries the `kit_presence` source marker. The requirement version pins the same kit and assignment event, preserving as-of history when a kit is moved or returned to stock. Legacy kit-list requirements and checks remain readable for historical records, but new obligations are never generated from those rows.

### 4.7 Configuration import and export [C] (owner decisions 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-13, [issue #129](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/129))

- **IMP-1** There are exactly two file types, each in **JSON or CSV**: a **kit library** file (one or more kit definitions with their contents), and a **vehicle** file (exactly one vehicle: its compartments and systems, items outside kits, and its kit list of definitions, compartments and required counts, KDEF-9). A CSV file states which type it is. Records are matched by stable keys that administrators maintain, never by database identifiers. Excel files are not supported in this release (OQ-69).
- **IMP-2** An import is first validated in full and previewed with **no writes**. The preview lists what would be created, changed or left unchanged, every conflict, and every error by file path or row. A file changes only what it names: nothing it omits is deleted or deactivated.
- **IMP-3** Confirming an import is one audited command with an operation id (idempotent on replay, REC-4) that applies everything or nothing. It writes **drafts** (kit definition versions, vehicle checklist versions, and the vehicle's kit list version) plus the vehicle's compartments, systems and kit instances; publication remains a separate explicit step (CHK-3, KDEF-2, KDEF-9). A vehicle file's required counts become that draft kit list. Where the vehicle has fewer active instances of a line's definition in its compartment than the count, the import creates the missing instances; it never deactivates or moves one (amended 2.0.0). Importing data identical to the current configuration changes nothing. An import whose preview was based on configuration that has since changed is refused rather than merged.
- **IMP-4** Supervisors and Administrators can download blank templates with synthetic examples for both file types and formats, generated from the same schema the importer validates, and exports of the current kit library or one selected vehicle in the same formats; an export re-imports unchanged. CSV output neutralizes spreadsheet-formula injection (RPT-2). Uploaded file contents are not written to logs or kept after the command; the audit records the actor, file type, summary counts and outcome.
- **IMP-5** Inspection schedules stay separate from equipment contents: a vehicle file names the checklist schedule it uses by key, or the importer uses the organization's existing default and shows it in the preview; files never define schedule rules.
- **IMP-6 [C] Keys and identity** (revised by amendment 5.0.0). The VIN remains the organization-unique vehicle key. Compartment/system position identifiers and checklist identifiers in a vehicle file are local to that file; they do not identify positions or checklists on another vehicle and are not organization-wide keys. Schedule slot names and kit-definition keys continue to refer to shared organization records. Database IDs are never file identifiers.
- **IMP-7 [C] Self-contained vehicle configuration** (revised by amendment 5.0.0). A vehicle file contains that vehicle's compartments and systems, its item requirements (CHK-11, amendment 6.0.0), complete vehicle-owned checklists, checklist-to-schedule assignments, references to shared kit definitions, its kit list and applicable schedule selections. Export omits physical kit instances and operational history, and preserves stable checklist precedence. Import preview resolves every included local reference, reports invalid/missing shared schedule or kit-definition references and shows unplaced checklist items. Importing as a new vehicle creates new vehicle-local position/checklist identities and remaps all internal references and many-to-many assignments while preserving stable checklist precedence; it does not bind a central checklist or link to another vehicle's positions. Duplicate routine anchors resolve under CHK-10 precedence. Existing atomicity, preview, audit, idempotency and non-destructive rules remain.
  - **Legacy files.** A pre-5.0 file containing organization-wide position keys or a template key is accepted only through a versioned compatibility path. Preview resolves the referenced legacy template and the source vehicle's effective override into one complete checklist per `(source vehicle, legacy template)` pair, preserves the generator's legacy template identity order (`template_id`) as stable checklist precedence and that checklist's bindings to all shared schedule slots, treats the file's position keys as local identifiers, and displays the resulting items, placements and assignments before any write. Confirmation imports independent vehicle checklists and remapped assignments. It creates no continuing template binding. A malformed or ambiguous legacy file is refused with no partial writes.

### 4.8 Configuration management [C] (owner decisions 2026-11-14, [issue #130](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/130))

Supervisors and Administrators maintain the fleet's configuration in the application (§3.3). Every change below is an audited command (AUD-1); history is never edited or deleted, and drafts are published under the existing rules (CHK-2, CHK-3, KDEF-2, KDEF-9).

- **CFG-1 Vehicles.** A vehicle is created with its VIN (IMP-6), a display name and an optional number; the name and number can later be edited. A vehicle is never deleted; it is deactivated, and it can be reactivated. Deactivation takes effect when recorded: from then on the vehicle owes no routine obligation, while its earlier obligations, checks, deficiencies and other history are kept. Reactivation takes effect when recorded, and obligations resume from then on; nothing is owed for the inactive period.
- **CFG-2 [C] Compartments and systems** (revised by amendment 5.0.0): retain the existing vehicle-owned revision, ordering, sealable and retirement behavior. Compartment/system identity and any position key are unique only within one vehicle; a key on one vehicle neither creates nor identifies a position on another. Any vehicle-checklist version or kit-list position reference resolves only against its own vehicle. Position history remains vehicle-scoped. A position cannot be retired while a checklist version assigned to that vehicle, in force or published for a later instant, or a kit list in force or published for a later instant, uses it; an active kit instance in the position also blocks retirement. The user must move the dependency or supersede it with a later publication. Publishing a checklist or kit list, or assigning a checklist, is refused if it would make a retired position required again. The refusal identifies the affected checklist and schedule assignment. Duplicate routine anchors are resolved by CHK-10 precedence, not refused.
- **CFG-3 [Withdrawn by amendment 5.0.0]:** duplicating a central checklist template is retired because checklists are vehicle-owned. Vehicle duplication remains under CFG-4 and copies independent checklist definitions and schedule assignments.
- **CFG-4 [C] Duplicating a vehicle** (revised by amendment 5.0.0): retain the current duplicate-vehicle behavior and exclusions (starts inactive; no operational history or physical kits copied). Copy the source vehicle's item requirements (CHK-11, amendment 6.0.0), independent checklists and all their schedule assignments, preserving their stable precedence, plus its vehicle-local positions; remap every checklist item-placement reference to the copied vehicle's local positions. The copy is independent after creation. Its assignments generate no obligations until activation (CFG-1).
- **CFG-5 Duplicating a kit instance.** This adds a new instance of the same kit definition (KDEF-3) at a chosen vehicle and compartment, with a new label. It copies no assignment, seal or check history. It is an ordinary kit addition (§3.3).
- **CFG-6 Preview and confirmation.** Before a duplication, retirement or deactivation is confirmed, the page shows what will be copied and what excluded, or which dependencies prevent it. Confirmation applies exactly what was shown, or is refused if the configuration has changed since.
- **CFG-7 [C] Assigning a vehicle checklist to a schedule slot** (revised by amendment 5.0.0). A Supervisor or Administrator may assign a vehicle-owned checklist to one shared schedule slot through an audited command with an operation id (REC-4). A checklist may have assignments to several slots, and a slot may have several checklists for its vehicle. Each assignment names one vehicle, checklist, schedule slot and effective start instant. The user may choose the current instant or a future station-local date and time; the server resolves that time using the station's configured time zone and established schedule/DST rules. Existing refusals for conflicting assignments of the same checklist to the same slot, missing schedule slots, and retired positions (CFG-2) continue to apply. Duplicate routine anchors resolve under CHK-10 precedence. An assignment may be recorded while its vehicle is inactive. It generates no obligations during an inactive interval: obligations begin only when both the assignment is in force and the vehicle is active. Thus an assignment already in force when a vehicle is activated owes obligations whose due instant is at or after activation; an assignment whose start is later owes those whose due instant is at or after that start. Deactivation and reactivation retain CFG-1 semantics, including no obligations during inactivity. This also applies to assignments copied by CFG-4: the duplicated vehicle remains obligation-free until activated. Assignment does not create or change schedule slots or their rules.
- **CFG-8 [C] End-assignment preview** (revised by amendment 5.0.0).
  - **Effect.** Ending an assignment takes effect at the instant it is confirmed. From that instant, no obligation with a due instant at or after it is generated from that assignment.
  - **Superseded obligations.** An already-created, unsatisfied obligation from the assignment is superseded, with the end of assignment as its cause, when both its due instant is at or after the end instant and its early window has not opened by then. A superseded obligation is not owed and is never shown as satisfied; it is not counted as Overdue or Missed (RPT-5a), and it does not affect readiness.
  - **Obligations that stay owed.** An obligation whose early window has already opened when the assignment ends keeps its ordinary state, as for any requirement change (§5.7). So do obligations due before the end instant, with their history.
  - **The preview.** Before confirmation, the page previews obligations that will be superseded and lists those already in their window as continuing. It states that ending an assignment satisfies no obligation, and that checks, deficiencies and audit history are retained.
  - **Confirmation.** The server recomputes, at the confirmation instant, the assignment in force for that vehicle, checklist and schedule slot, and both lists. If any differs from the preview, nothing changes and a fresh preview is shown. The end-assignment command is audited and idempotent on replay using its operation id (REC-4).
- **CFG-9 [C] Vehicle checklist editing and publication.** A Supervisor or Administrator may draft, edit, place or unplace an item, and publish a checklist version for one vehicle. The command is authorized, audited and idempotent under the existing command rules. A placement must name a current, non-retired position on that same vehicle or be null. Publication is effective no earlier than recorded and preserves earlier versions and due-instant resolution across all schedule assignments. Item membership/placement edits affect no other vehicle. Shared schedule definitions and kit definitions are not copied into vehicle-owned data.

## 5. Inspection obligations, windows and scheduling

### 5.1 Concepts

- **Requirement** — a rule saying "this item, in this place on this vehicle, must be checked on this schedule" (from a published vehicle checklist version assigned to a shared schedule slot, the vehicle item requirement it inspects (CHK-11), plus fleet schedule configuration; amendments 5.0.0 and 6.0.0).
- **Obligation** — one concrete, dated instance of a requirement: `(vehicle, item requirement, kit instance, due instant, requirement version)`, where the item requirement identifies the item and its place on the vehicle (CHK-11; for an item inside a kit, the kit definition's content entry) and the kit instance exists only for an item inside a kit (§4.2). Obligations are what checks satisfy. Obligations created before amendment 6.0.0 keep their original key and are read through the §18.3 map.
- **Check** — an individually attributed record of one outcome for one item (§7).

**5.1.1 [C]** Compliance is determined at the **obligation × item** level, per item requirement (one stock of the item in its current place, CHK-11) and, for an item inside a kit, per kit instance. A session being complete, or all crew having participated, is never itself evidence of compliance (REQ §2.3, HO inv. 1).

### 5.3 Window semantics [OQ-02: working model accepted by owner 2026-10-15; detailed edge cases remain provisional]

The owner has not defined exactly when an unmet check is "Due" vs "Overdue". **[P] working model**, to be confirmed:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `D` | due instant (e.g. 06:00 local) |
| `E = D − w_early` | earliest qualifying instant (w_early default 1 h) |
| `G = D + w_grace` | grace deadline (w_grace default 1 h) |
| `L = G + w_late` | late-completion limit (w_late default 12 h) |

| Obligation state | Condition (server time `t`, obligation unsatisfied) | Display |
|---|---|---|
| Upcoming | `t < E` | Not shown as actionable |
| **Current** | `E ≤ t < D` | "Current" — may be checked now |
| **Due / Grace Period** | `D ≤ t ≤ G` | "Due" (grace running) |
| **Overdue** | `t > G` | "Overdue" |
| Satisfied | a qualifying check exists (§5.4) | "Satisfied" with who/when |

Rules **[P]**:
- **SCH-1** A check is **on time** for an obligation if its server-recorded time `t_c` satisfies `E ≤ t_c ≤ G`. An on-time check made before `D` counts as an early check for that obligation.
- **SCH-2** For each inspection or odometer obligation, `L` is fixed when the obligation is created as `G + w_late` using the agency setting captured at that creation. A check at `G < t_c ≤ L` is a **late completion** under the existing eligibility and ordering rules. It is linked to every obligation of the same vehicle and item requirement (and kit instance) that is still unsatisfied, past `G` and not past its `L`, together with every on-time one (SCH-6; amendment 6.0.0). Late Pass, Fail, and Unable follow the same §5.4 rules as on-time ones, so a late Pass or Fail satisfies the *past* obligation, which stays permanently recorded as *Overdue → Completed late* with `t_c` and the lateness interval (history and reports). It clears the *current* overdue indicator because the item has now been checked. An obligation still unsatisfied after `L` can no longer be completed: it stays permanently Overdue (missed) in history and reports, and only its next scheduled obligation can be checked. A check that is neither on time nor within `L` of an unsatisfied obligation links to nothing and counts only as an ordinary recorded check (participation, history). How a missed obligation is counted in the fleet-readiness summary (Overdue and Missed) is specified in RPT-5a. A setting change affects only obligations created afterwards; it does not change the late limit or linking eligibility of an existing obligation.
- **SCH-3** `w_early` and `w_grace` remain separately configurable. `w_late` is one agency-wide server setting, default 12 h. Its value is captured permanently on each newly created inspection or odometer obligation. Existing synthetic obligations are assigned 12 h by migration. Changes apply to subsequently created obligations.
- **SCH-4** State is always derived from `(rule, checks, server time)`, never stored as authoritative.

### 5.4 What satisfies an obligation [C: owner decisions of 2026-10-15 for Failed and Unable to Inspect]

| Outcome | Satisfies the obligation? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Passed (valid) | **Yes** — on time, or as a late completion under SCH-2 | [C] |
| Not Applicable | **Yes, only if** an administrator-defined NA rule applies | [C] (REQ §2.3). Displayed as "Not applicable (rule …)", not as Passed. |
| Failed | **Yes — the check was performed [C, owner decision 2026-10-15].** The obligation shows "Inspected — failed", the deficiency is raised, and the item's attention state stays red until verified (PRN-6). | A failed item is not a missed inspection. |
| Unable to Inspect | **Not by itself [C, owner decision 2026-10-15].** A reason (and deficiency linkage) is mandatory. The obligation stays unsatisfied until a **supervisor marks it complete** (see 5.4.1). | Never displayed as Passed. |
| Invalidated check | **No** — excluded from compliance [C] | Statuses recalculated. |
| Superseded by author correction | The *correction's* outcome is the effective outcome [C] | Original retained. |

#### 5.4.1 Supervisor completion of an Unable-to-Inspect obligation [C intent; P mechanics]

- **UTI-1 [C]** A crew member recording Unable to Inspect must give a reason; the obligation then remains open and visible to supervisors.
- **UTI-2 [C]** A supervisor (or administrator) may mark that obligation **complete**. **[P]** Mechanics: an append-only `obligation_determination` of type `SUPERVISOR_COMPLETED` carrying actor, instant, mandatory explanation, and a reference to the Unable-to-Inspect check(s). It is distinct from a Pass: reports and the UI show "Completed by supervisor — unable to inspect" (RPT-7). It does not close or alter the linked deficiency and does not change operational status.
- **UTI-3 [C: owner-approved 2026-10-15, OQ-45]** The determination is itself invalidatable by an administrator with an explanation; the original is retained. If the underlying Unable-to-Inspect check is later **invalidated**, the supervisor determination is voided with it and the obligation returns to unsatisfied. If its author **corrects** it to Pass/Fail, the corrected outcome governs per §5.4 and the supervisor determination remains in history as superseded. Either way the linked deficiency is unaffected (INV-3). **[C: owner-decided 2026-10-25, OQ-41]** A supervisor may complete an obligation whose Unable-to-Inspect check they recorded themselves; reports flag that case.

### 5.5 Overlaps and multiple obligations [OQ-03]

- **SCH-5 [C: owner decision 2026-11-21, amendment 6.0.0]** Link cardinality: `check_obligation_link` rows are unique per `(check_id, obligation_id)`. A check links to every obligation SCH-6 makes eligible, and each link records whether it was on time or late. Every link of one check is to an obligation of the same vehicle, item requirement and kit instance. Shared-satisfaction rules and their links recorded before 6.0.0 remain in history; no new rule can be created.
- **SCH-6 [C: owner decisions 2026-11-21, amendment 6.0.0] One check completes the item-place wherever it is due.** A check links to every unsatisfied obligation of the same vehicle and item requirement (CHK-11) for which `E ≤ t_c ≤ G` or `G < t_c ≤ L`, whichever vehicle checklist created it, including an earlier obligation of the same checklist. Eligibility depends only on obligations and time, never on which sessions are open (§6.1). A check never links to another item requirement's obligation, so a check never completes the same item kept in another place at the same time. A moved item requirement stays one requirement (CHK-11): a check at its current place completes its obligations pinned to a version with an earlier place. Invalidating or correcting the check re-derives all its links together (§19.1). For an item inside a kit only the obligations of the kit instance the check names are eligible; a check never links to another instance's obligation. A kit presence check is eligible only for obligations of its own kit-list line (KDEF-12). Each linked obligation follows §5.4 for the check's outcome. Required tests: overlapping windows across two checklists (AC-30), on-time plus late links from one check, two identical kit instances with one checked, two places of one item with one checked, and invalidation removing all links.
- **SCH-7 [P]** Repeated checks in one window are all retained; the earliest valid qualifying check satisfies the obligation, and later ones contribute to participation counts and history (REQ §2.3).

### 5.6 Conditional obligations [C / P] (REQ §2.2)

Some obligations are omitted when stated conditions hold. **[P]** Conditions are expressed as a closed, versioned set of rule types, **not** free-form expressions (no `eval`; see [engineering standards](docs/engineering-standards.md#core-principles)). The initial rule type is:

**`CARRY_OVER_FROM_PRIOR_SHIFT`** — the obligation at `D` is *not required* if (a) every person on the effective roster at `D` was also on the effective roster for the immediately preceding shift that ends at `D`, **and** (b) a qualifying, valid, non-invalidated check for the same item was completed during that preceding shift. Example (synthetic): the 06:00 glucometer check is omitted when all crew working at 06:00 were on the midnight–06:00 shift and the midnight glucometer check passed. If **any** crew member begins duty at `D`, the obligation is required. [C] example from REQ §2.2.

Rules **[P]**:
- **CND-1** The evaluation records its inputs (rule version, roster snapshot and its source/age, the check relied on) and its result as an immutable **obligation determination** event, so that an audit can reconstruct *why* an obligation was waived.
- **CND-2** "Effective roster" = scheduled roster (Humanity, §15.2) overlaid with the actual-roster correction(s) that govern the time (§15.3, including how concurrent corrections are resolved).
- **CND-3** Fail closed: if the roster is unavailable/stale beyond the staleness limit (§15.2), or the determination cannot be computed, the obligation is **required**.
- **CND-4** A waived obligation displays as "Not required — carried over (rule …)", never as Passed.
- **CND-5** A later roster change that invalidates the waiver reinstates the obligation (re-evaluation is an event, not an in-place edit). If that reinstatement occurs after `G`, it is shown overdue from `G`; the timing consequence is OQ-37.
- **CND-6** New rule types require a spec amendment (§25).
- **CND-7 [C: owner-decided 2026-10-23, OQ-51]** Re-evaluating an existing waiver (CND-5) reinstates the obligation only when something newer than the waiver's evidence invalidates it within its dependency window (the preceding shift through `D`): a roster change, including an actual-roster correction, that breaks condition (a) or leaves no preceding shift ending at `D`; a refresh reporting roster data unavailable for that window; disagreeing corrections (§15.3); or the relied-on check being invalidated or no longer qualifying for condition (b). Roster data that has only aged past the staleness limit (HUM-3) without changing does **not** reinstate a waiver granted on fresh data; the validity of the relied-on inspection is what matters. The staleness limit still governs new determinations: data older than the limit at evaluation time cannot create a waiver (CND-3).

### 5.7 Which requirement version governs [OQ-05]

**[P]** An obligation is created bound to the requirement version **effective at its due instant `D`**. Consequences:
- Publishing a version with a future effective date does not change obligations already due before that date.
- A session's pinned checklist version (CHK-4) governs what the crew member *sees and records* in that session; the obligations used for *current* due/overdue status are the ones bound at creation, resolved as in this rule. The UI shows both (original version and current obligations) and never injects new items into an old session silently.
- An immediately-effective publish takes effect for obligations whose `D` is at or after the publish instant; for obligations already created and in-window it does not apply: the administrator option "apply to open obligations" is **not offered** in this release. **[C: owner-decided 2026-10-23, OQ-52]** The owner may reconsider this in a future revision; offering the option would need an amendment (§25.4) that also settles its audit and its effect on checks already recorded against the open obligation.

**5.7.1 [C] Vehicle checklist resolution (amendment 5.0.0).** Resolution uses every vehicle checklist version assigned to vehicle `V` and shared schedule slot `S` at due instant `D`. Each checklist contributes its own item requirements and each obligation pins the effective checklist version under the existing §5.7 rules. Item records remain organization-wide; vehicle checklists reference them. If several assigned checklist versions require the same `(vehicle, item requirement, kit instance, D)` routine anchor, CHK-10 selects one effective requirement deterministically by schedule-slot text value under code-point ordering, then stable checklist precedence within that vehicle (the legacy `template_id` ordering). The selected requirement version is pinned to the single routine obligation for that anchor. This codifies the existing generator's deterministic resolution and prevents competing obligations without rejecting later checklist, schedule or kit changes. Checks link to obligations with distinct due instants under SCH-6. A non-kit item requirement whose place is Not placed still receives its normal obligation. From amendment 6.0.0 the place is part of the item requirement's identity (CHK-11): a check names the item requirement shown to the inspector, and it never satisfies another place's obligation. Its check, correction, deficiency, report and as-of history retain the original vehicle and checklist version.

The shared kit definitions, kit-list placement/count behavior (KDEF-9–KDEF-12) and kit assignment rules (TIME-1a) remain unchanged. A vehicle-checklist migration or edit never changes a kit instance assignment. When a kit assignment changes the contents relied on by an intact seal, apply the existing CAB-4/OQ-72 rule: the seal no longer supports reliance until the compartment is inspected and resealed. Record kit moves in the field so TIME-1a obligations follow the actual assignment.

### 5.8 Generation and time basis [P / OQ-25]

- **TIME-1** Obligations are materialized by a scheduled job ahead of time (default horizon: through the next 48 h) and are idempotent on `(vehicle, item, kit instance, D, requirement_version)`; a missed job run is repaired by the next run, never by duplicate rows.
- **TIME-1a [C: owner-decided 2026-10-24, OQ-53, OQ-54]** For an item inside a kit the generator materializes one obligation per kit instance that is active on the vehicle, in the item's kit slot, at that `D`; with no such instance it materializes none. Which instances those are is read from the kit assignment record (`kit_assignment_event`, §18), never from the mutable `kit_instance` row, so the result is the same when recomputed as-of (REG-6). An instance added later is required only for due instants at or after its recorded activation: an inspection whose window is already open at that moment is not required for it, and earlier instants are not backfilled (as for return to service, §5.9). An instance removed, deactivated or moved to another vehicle stops being required from the recorded removal instant: its obligations with a due instant after the removal are retired, and earlier ones keep their state, so a removal can never erase a missed inspection; the history is kept as-of. A move between vehicles is a removal from one vehicle plus an addition to the other, and a temporary absence (for example restocking) is a gap with no backfill. An assignment takes effect at or after the instant it is recorded and is never backdated, as for requirement bindings (owner-approved 2026-10-24). A missing kit instance (an item outside a kit) is compared as a value, so two obligations cannot differ only by a missing instance.
- **TIME-2** All instants are stored in UTC; schedules are defined in the station's IANA time zone (single configured zone; exact zone OQ-25). `D` is computed from local wall-clock time.
- **TIME-3** Daylight-saving rules **[P]**: a nonexistent local time (spring forward) resolves to the first valid instant after the gap; an ambiguous local time (fall back) resolves to its **first** occurrence. Window arithmetic is on absolute instants, so a window may span 59–61 minutes' wall time on a transition day.
- **TIME-4** Server time is authoritative for every timestamp used in compliance; client clocks are never trusted (REQ §9).

### 5.9 Out-of-service exemption [C] (REQ §2.2)

While a vehicle's operational status is **Out of Service**, routine obligations are not generated for that interval; the interval is displayed as "Exempt — out of service", distinct from Pass. Service status and resumption are explicit, audited supervisor actions (§9). A change of crew, or equipment use after a call, does **not** itself create an obligation to repeat an entire vehicle inspection [C].

**[OQ-31]** Whether returning to service requires an inspection before the next scheduled obligation. **[P]** Safe default: no automatic obligation; the supervisor may require an ad hoc inspection, recorded as an audited, scoped obligation.

**ADH-1 [C: owner-approved 2026-10-30, [issue #158](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/158)].** A Supervisor or Administrator may explicitly require a one-off inspection of selected checklist items on a vehicle, with a mandatory explanation and future due instant `D`. Each selected item names its physical kit instance, or no kit. The request pins the published requirement effective at `D`, using only configuration already recorded when requested. Its window opens when the request is recorded (`E`), ends at `D` (`G = D`, no additional grace), and is never retroactive. Each request has a separate identity, even at the same vehicle/item/kit/due instant as a routine obligation or another request; it is not in the routine selection stream. Subsequent generation, publication reconciliation and kit changes do not replace or retire the explicit request. It is non-routine, so an Out-of-Service interval does not exempt it; carry-over waivers do not apply. Satisfaction and link cardinality follow §5.3–§5.5: a check satisfies the request together with every other obligation of the same item requirement that SCH-6 makes eligible (amendment 6.0.1). Returning to service alone creates nothing.

## 6. Inspection sessions

### 6.1 Role of a session [C]

A session organizes work; it **never determines compliance** (PRN-1). Compliance derives only from valid checks linked to obligations. Sessions group a vehicle, a checklist version, participants, an optional actual-roster correction, and the checks/odometer entries made within them.

### 6.2 States [P]

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Active; checks may be recorded. |
| **Incomplete** | Auto-closed by inactivity (§6.3), ended at its resume deadline (SES-8), or manually ended with unmet items. Progress preserved. |
| **Closed** | Ended by a participant when all items in its checklist version have a valid current outcome, or ended explicitly. Informational only. |

A session is **in progress** while it is Open and the current time is before its end instant (SES-9): it has reached neither its inactivity limit (SES-1) nor its resume deadline (SES-8). Reopening moves Incomplete (or Closed) → Open, within SES-3, SES-7 and SES-8, and is an audited event [C]. **[C: owner decisions 2026-11-21, amendment 6.0.0]** Each vehicle checklist has at most one session in progress (SES-7). Sessions of different checklists on one vehicle may be in progress together, and an item requirement they share is completed by one check (SCH-6). **[C: owner decision 2026-10-24, [issue #78](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/78)]** Only a participant may reopen that same ended session or manually end it. A nonparticipant joins the session in progress for that checklist, or starts a new session when none is in progress.

### 6.3 Inactivity auto-close, resume deadline and one session per checklist [C / OQ-04, OQ-81, OQ-82]

- **SES-1 [C]** A session with no activity for **6 hours** closes automatically as **Incomplete**, preserving every check and progress record.
- **SES-2 [OQ-04 → P]** "Activity" means a **persisted business event** in the session: a committed check, correction, odometer entry, deficiency report or link made from the session, roster confirmation, or reopen. Passive viewing, scrolling, websocket/heartbeat traffic, and failed/unacknowledged saves do **not** reset the timer. Reopening starts a new 6-hour window from the reopen instant; that window never extends past the session's resume deadline (SES-8, amendment 6.0.0).
- **SES-3 [C: owner decisions 2026-10-24, [issue #78](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/78), and 2026-11-21, amendment 6.0.0]** A participant may reopen a Closed or Incomplete session without supervisor approval, before its resume deadline (SES-8) and only while no other session of the same vehicle checklist is in progress (SES-7). The reopen is audit-logged (actor, instant, session). A refused reopen changes nothing and is audited as a refused command. A nonparticipant joins the session in progress for that checklist, or starts a new session when none is in progress.
- **SES-4 [C]** Reopening refreshes current requirement statuses; no "what changed" summary is required. Completed checks keep their original timestamps and satisfy a *current* obligation only if still valid and within that obligation's window.
- **SES-5 [C]** Work may be resumed by another crew member without duplicating valid completed checks.
- **SES-6 [P]** The auto-close job is idempotent and tolerant of delay: closing is stamped with the *activity-based* close instant (last activity + 6 h), not the job's wake time, so the audit record is correct even if the job runs late.
- **SES-7 [C: owner decisions 2026-11-21, amendment 6.0.0] One session in progress per vehicle checklist.** For each vehicle checklist (its identity, not a version), at most one session is in progress. Starting that checklist while one is in progress joins it, even when it pins an earlier version (CHK-4). Nobody can start a second or separate session of that checklist, and nobody can reopen another session of it. Any participant may end it (§3.3). Starting a different checklist on the same vehicle is unaffected. Session commands for one vehicle are serialized: two simultaneous starts of one checklist produce one session, and a start racing a reopen leaves exactly one in progress.
- **SES-8 [C: owner decisions 2026-11-21, amendment 6.0.0] Resume limit.** Each organization has a resume limit that an Administrator sets in whole hours from 1 to 24 (default 6). The current value is captured on each session when it opens, so a later change never moves an existing session's deadline. The session's resume deadline is `R = opened_at + captured limit`. Joins, activity and reopens never move it. From `R` on, the session cannot be reopened, joined or recorded into by anyone; for a session opened before 6.0.0, read `R` as its effective deadline `R*` (SES-9). If it is still Open, it ends as Incomplete at `R` even if it is in active use, recorded under SES-9. Its records are kept permanently and stay readable (AUD-1, AUD-5). Work continues in a new session, which pins the current version (CHK-4) and shows earlier checks through current obligations (SES-4, SES-5, PRN-1). There is no override. With a limit of 6 h or less an Open session always reaches `R` before its inactivity limit, so only a session ended by hand can be reopened; with a longer limit an idle-closed session can be reopened until `R`.
- **SES-9 [P] Enforcement and recording.** Let `A` be the session's last persisted activity (SES-2) and its **effective deadline** `R* = max(R, A)`. For a session opened under 6.0.0 no activity can be recorded at or after `R`, so `R* = R`; for a session opened before 6.0.0, activity recorded after `R` under the earlier rules remains valid history. The session's **end instant** is `min(A + 6 h, R*)`. Reads, refusals, projections, database guards and the job all use this one instant: the session is in progress only while it is Open and the current time is before it; a participant may reopen an ended session only before `R*`; no check, join or reopen is accepted at or after `R*`. Enforcement never waits for the job. The idempotent, restart-safe job (ARC-4) appends the ending event at exactly the end instant, never at the time it runs: `AUTO_INCOMPLETE` if `A + 6 h ≤ R*` (SES-6), otherwise `DEADLINE_INCOMPLETE` at `R*`.

### 6.4 Odometer and engine hours [C / OQ-12, OQ-18]

- **ODO-1 [C]** Every routine ambulance inspection requires an odometer entry.
- **ODO-2 [OQ-12 → P]** Because no single crew member "completes" a session, odometer is modeled as a **vehicle-level odometer obligation attached to each scheduled routine inspection** (same window as that inspection's obligations; any crew member may satisfy it; one entry per obligation; others see it satisfied). Crew members are not each required to re-enter it.
- **ODO-3 [C]** Anomalous readings (**[P]** lower than the previous valid reading; or exceeding the previous by more than a configurable plausibility threshold) require correction or explicit confirmation, recorded with who confirmed. **[P]** Until an administrator sets one, the plausibility threshold is **1,000 miles (1,600 km)** between consecutive valid readings (a reading exactly at the limit is normal); every change is kept as a numbered revision (AUD-4). A vehicle's readings keep one unit (mi or km). Engine hours (ODO-4) have no threshold: only a value lower than the previous valid one needs confirmation (OQ-55). When an author corrects an earlier odometer or engine-hours reading, if the replacement value would exceed the next effective reading in the vehicle's sequence (making that later reading lower than the corrected value), the correction requires explicit confirmation, recorded with who confirmed and retained in the append-only history. Both readings and their sequence positions remain unchanged; later readings are not silently rewritten or invalidated (**[C: owner decision 2026-10-26, [issue #125](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/125), OQ-58]**).
- **ODO-4 [C]** Engine hours may be entered but are **not mandatory** in the initial release [OQ-18 for when this changes].
- **ODO-5 [P]** Odometer entries are append-only records subject to the same author-only correction and administrator-invalidation rules as checks.

### 6.5 Views and filters [C] (REQ §2.4)

- **VW-1** Switchable **Priority** (attention-needed first) and **Normal** (equipment/compartment order) views. Each crew member's preferred view persists across sessions **and devices**. (The decided list in OQ says "per EMT and device"; REQ §2.4 says "across devices". **[P]** per-crew-member, synchronized across devices; reconcile if the owner meant a per-device override — OQ-38.)
- **VW-2** A **Needs Attention Only** filter works in either view and is remembered per crew member. It includes actionable due, overdue, incomplete and unresolved items **excluding** supervisor-suspended routine checks, which stay visible in full checklists, deficiency reporting and dashboards.
- **VW-3** Relevant known deficiencies are shown **beside their checklist items before inspection**, and the vehicle's operational status is prominent.
- **VW-4** QR/barcode entry points for vehicle and kit, with manual navigation fallback. QR payloads **[P]** carry only an opaque identifier, never a credential, and entry still requires authentication.
- **VW-5 [C: owner decision 2026-11-21, amendment 6.0.0]** An item requirement appears in every vehicle checklist that lists it, in that checklist's order. Its obligation and attention state are shared by all of those checklists, so a check recorded in one session shows in the others without a manual refresh (§17.3). An item kept in two places appears as two rows, each in its own place.

## 7. Recording checks, concurrency and corrections

### 7.1 Outcomes [C] (REQ §2.3)

**Passed**, **Failed**, **Unable to Inspect**, **Not Applicable** (only where an administrator-defined rule is valid). Unable to Inspect requires an explanation and deficiency linkage (create or link). An outcome that is invalid for the item or that violates its rule must be rejected server-side and does not satisfy anything.

### 7.2 The check record [P]

An append-only record:

```
check_id (server-generated), operation_id (client UUID, unique per org), organization_id,
vehicle_id, item_id, checklist_version_id (nullable only for a standalone kit-presence check, KDEF-12), session_id (nullable),
obligation_id (nullable, via link), kit_instance_id (required for an item inside a kit, else null), asset identity at time (nullable),
kit_requirement_id (required for a kit presence check, KDEF-12, else null),
item_requirement_id (required for a check of an item outside a kit, CHK-11, taken from the row shown, never inferred; amendment 6.0.0),
outcome, observed_value/quantity (nullable), explanation (nullable),
actor_user_id, actor_roles_at_time, roster_context_ref,
server_recorded_at, client_observed_at (informational),
bulk_group_id (nullable; reserved, unused in R1), basis_check_id (see 7.4),
acknowledges_check_id (nullable, CON-3), purpose (ROUTINE | VERIFICATION), verification_for_deficiency_id (nullable),
state: EFFECTIVE | SUPERSEDED | INVALIDATED  (derived from event rows, see 7.8)
```

Rows are never updated or deleted by application code; state changes are separate append-only event rows (correction, invalidation).

### 7.3 Auto-save and acknowledgement [C] (REQ §2.3, §9)

- **REC-1** Tapping an outcome saves immediately; there is no Save/Submit button.
- **REC-2** The UI shows each item as **Pending** (sent, unacknowledged), **Saved** (server acknowledged), or **Failed** (error, with retry). Only a **server acknowledgement** means the entry is committed.
- **REC-3** All viewers receive updated statuses without manual refresh, within the freshness targets of §17.3. If the live channel is lost, the UI says so prominently and shows data as possibly stale [OQ-15 for wording].
- **REC-4 [P]** Each client write attempt carries an **operation id** (UUID) — the idempotency key. A client retries an unacknowledged write **only with the same operation id**. The server enforces `UNIQUE(organization_id, operation_id)` and returns the originally committed result for a replay. A transport failure is treated as ambiguous (per [engineering standards](docs/engineering-standards.md#safe-administrative-automation)): the UI shows "unconfirmed" and retries with the same id until the server answers. **Lost response:** the replay returns the stored committed result; if nothing had committed, the server evaluates the write fresh. A conflict warning (CON-3) is **not** a commit and does not consume the operation id; an identical retry is re-evaluated against current state.

### 7.4 Concurrent checking and duplicate warning [C / OQ-10]

- **CON-1 [C]** Different crew members may check different items concurrently. There is **no locking** of items.
- **CON-2 [C]** If a crew member is about to record an item that another crew member has just recorded, the system **warns and offers to proceed or cancel**. If they proceed, **both** records are preserved independently.
- **CON-3 [OQ-10 → P]** "Just recorded" is defined by *the viewer's basis*, not a wall-clock guess: each write carries `basis_check_id` = the newest effective check the client had displayed for that item+obligation. If the server's newest effective check for that item+obligation differs (a newer one exists), the server **rejects the attempt with a conflict warning** containing the newer check (who, when, outcome). The user's decision to proceed is a *new decision* and a *new* operation id, submitted with `acknowledges_check_id` = the id of the newer check shown in the warning. At commit the server compares `acknowledges_check_id` with the then-newest effective check: if still equal, the independent record commits; if a **further** check landed between warning and acknowledgment, the server returns a fresh warning for that newer check and commits nothing. A check from the same actor as the newer one is not a conflict. For an item inside a kit the comparison is per kit instance: a newer check on another instance is not a conflict. Required tests: lost response on first attempt, replay after warning, and a concurrent check between warning and acknowledgment (AC-31).
- **CON-4 [P]** The conflict warning applies to every outcome including Fail; a Fail is never silently dropped because a Pass raced it (PRN-6). If a Pass and a Fail for the same item both commit, both are retained: the obligation is satisfied/inspected per §5.4, the item's attention state remains red while the failure's deficiency is unresolved.
- **CON-5 [P]** Server commits are serialized per `(vehicle, item)` by a database transaction so that CON-3's comparison and the insert are atomic.
- **CON-6 [OQ-15]** Exact warning text, stale-view handling, and whether to indicate another crew member currently viewing an item are open; presence indicators are not part of R1 unless the owner decides otherwise.
- **CON-7 [C: amendment 6.0.0]** For CON-2, CON-3 and CON-5, "item" means the item requirement (one stock of the item in its current place, CHK-11) and, for an item inside a kit, the kit instance. The comparison covers every obligation of that item requirement, so two crew members recording the same item-place from sessions of different checklists see each other's checks.

### 7.5 Pass-then-fail and fail-then-pass [P]

Statuses use **two independent dimensions** (PRN-3, PRN-6):
- **Obligation state** (§5.3/§5.4): Satisfied / Inspected–failed / Current / Due / Overdue …
- **Item attention state**: *Clear* only if no unresolved (open or pending-verification) deficiency applies to the item, and the latest effective check is not Failed/Unable. Otherwise *Attention*, regardless of later passing checks. **[C: owner decision 2026-11-21, amendment 6.0.0]** For an item outside a kit, "the item" here and in §8 is the item requirement (one stock of the item in its current place, CHK-11): a failure or deficiency in one place never makes the same item in another place need attention.

A later Pass does not close a deficiency. Only §8 transitions do.

### 7.6 Bulk pass [C / OQ-11] — deferred from R1

**Deferred [C]** (owner decision 2026-10-20, OQ-11). Bulk Pass is not part of R1: no R1 UI, API or acceptance test is required, and `bulk_group_id` / item `bulk_eligible` are reserved and unused in R1. BLK-1–BLK-5 are retained unchanged as the design to use if a later release restores the feature. The requirement text below is not withdrawn.

- **BLK-1 [C]** A crew member may select eligible items and mark them Passed in one action. The system persists **separate attributed check records** per item (sharing a `bulk_group_id`).
- **BLK-2 [C]** Ineligible: items requiring direct measurement or special observation, items with an open/pending deficiency (bulk pass must not override a deficiency), and items under supervisor suspension (must not bypass suspended items).
- **BLK-3 [OQ-11 → P]** Eligibility is an explicit boolean on the item definition (default **false**). Confirmation is **required for every bulk action** (summary: vehicle, number of items, listed names) until the owner decides otherwise.
- **BLK-4 [P]** Bulk submission is processed per item inside one request: items with a newer effective check than the client's basis (CON-3) are **excluded and returned** as conflicts for individual decision; the rest commit. The response lists committed, conflicted and rejected items. A concurrent Fail on an item always wins attention state (§7.5).
- **BLK-5 [P]** A bulk request carries one idempotency key per item (derived deterministically from the request key), so replay cannot duplicate any item.

### 7.7 Corrections by the original author [C] (REQ §2.3)

- **COR-1** Only the original author may correct a check. Supervisors and administrators cannot correct another user's check; a supervisor may record a **fresh independent** check instead. Reason is optional.
- **COR-2** A correction is a **new linked record**; the original is retained and marked superseded. The effective outcome is the correction's. Attribution of both is kept.
- **COR-3 [C]** If the author corrects an *accidentally entered* Failed to Passed, the linked deficiency is marked **Reported in Error** (history retained). This baseline rule is confirmed; the extra conditions in COR-4 are owner-approved (OQ-42, 2026-10-15).
- **COR-4 [OQ-07, OQ-08, OQ-09; additional conditions approved by owner 2026-10-15 (OQ-42)]** Two distinct user flows: **"Correct entry"** (fix a data-entry mistake) and **"Record repair/verification"** (§8.6). Correction never implies repair. Additional conditions (owner-approved 2026-10-15): "Reported in Error" is applied only when the correction is a Failed→Passed through *Correct entry*, no *other* effective Failed or Unable to Inspect check supports the deficiency (an item that could not be inspected is unverified, not a resolved defect), and no corrective action or verification has been recorded for it. Otherwise the correction is still recorded, the deficiency stays open, and a supervisor is notified to resolve it.
- **COR-5 [OQ-32]** Whether corrections are time-limited (for example, only until the obligation window closes or until deficiency work begins) is undecided; no limit is applied by default.
- **COR-6 [P]** A correction itself may be corrected by the same author; the chain is preserved.

### 7.8 Administrator invalidation [C] (REQ §2.3)

- **INV-1** Only administrators may invalidate another user's check (or odometer entry). **Mandatory explanation.** The original data is preserved, never overwritten or deleted.
- **INV-2** An invalidated check is excluded from compliance and all statuses are recalculated (derivation is reproducible, §19).
- **INV-2a [C: owner-approved 2026-10-15, OQ-43]** *Chain semantics.* A correction chain (original plus the author's corrections) is one logical check. An administrator invalidates **the chain**, whichever row they select; the entire chain is excluded and **no earlier outcome is revived** (reviving an old, possibly accidental Failed or Passed would fabricate a result no one currently stands behind). If the chain was invalidated, a later correction by the author is refused (they record a fresh check). If an administrator wants only to disregard a correction, they invalidate the chain and a fresh check is recorded. Downstream recompute: obligation links of the chain are voided (obligations re-derived); deficiencies supported by the chain stay open (INV-3) with the chain marked invalid in their evidence list; if the chain was a verification, INV-4 applies; any as-of report at `T` before the invalidation shows the chain as then effective (REG-6).
- **INV-3 (critical) [C]** Invalidating a Failed check **does not close or cancel its deficiency**. The deficiency stays open until it is explicitly closed under §8.8: an Administrator may close it generally, and a Supervisor may close it only when its current correction-time verification scope is empty.
- **INV-4 [C: owner accepted proposal 2026-10-15]** Invalidating a *verification* check that had caused automatic closure **reopens** the deficiency (fail toward open) and notifies its assignee and supervisors.
- **INV-5 [OQ-33]** Whether an administrator can invalidate their own check is undecided; **[P]** default: no — they use correction.
- **INV-6 [P]** Invalidation is not undoable; a mistaken invalidation is addressed by a new fresh check and a supervisor/administrator note, never by editing the invalidation row.
- **INV-7 [C: owner-approved 2026-10-20, OQ-48, [issue #31](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/31)]** If an author's Failed→Passed Correct-entry marked a deficiency **Reported in Error** under COR-3/COR-4, later administrator invalidation of that correction chain **reopens that deficiency to Open**. The reopening is an attributed lifecycle event in the same transaction as the invalidation; the invalidated chain remains in its evidence list marked invalid. No earlier check outcome revives, no repair is inferred, and operational status is unchanged. This applies to the deficiency still in Reported in Error from that chain, not an independently administrator-closed deficiency. The owner selected "Reopen" and explicitly approved amendment 1.3.0 on 2026-10-20 (§25.6).

## 8. Deficiency lifecycle

### 8.1 Entity [C] (REQ §4)

A deficiency concerns an item (for an item outside a kit, one item requirement: the item in one place, CHK-11, amendment 6.0.0), kit, compartment, or the whole vehicle; may be reported from an inspection or independently; has category/severity, photos/documents, assignee, deadline, and a timestamped progress history. A deficiency is an independent record: it is **separate from the failed check** that may have spawned it, and correction/verification/closure records are separate from that check (REQ §4, HO inv. 5).

### 8.2 Status model [P]

Status is derived from the latest lifecycle event; events are append-only.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open** | Reported, not yet being worked or newly reopened. |
| **In Progress** | Assigned/worked. (Folded into Open if the owner prefers fewer states.) |
| **Awaiting External Dependency** | Waiting on a third party (§8.5). |
| **Pending Verification** | A correction was recorded; verification not yet passed. |
| **Closed — Verified** | Closed automatically by successful verification. |
| **Closed — Administrator** | Closed explicitly by an administrator without verification (explanation required). |
| **Closed — No Current Verification Items** | Closed explicitly because checklist or kit changes left no current item to verify (explanation required). |
| **Reported in Error** | Cancelled via COR-3/COR-4 (or later-defined OQ-07 paths). |
| **Reopened** | Event; status returns to Open (§8.7). |

Allowed transitions **[P]**:

```
Open ──assign/start──▶ In Progress ──correction recorded──▶ Pending Verification
Open/InProgress ◀──▶ Awaiting External Dependency
Pending Verification ──verification pass (all scope)──▶ Closed—Verified   (automatic)
Pending Verification ──verification attempt fails──▶ In Progress
any open status ──administrator close (+explanation)──▶ Closed—Administrator
Open/In Progress/Awaiting External Dependency ──supervisor/administrator close when current scope is empty (+explanation)──▶ Closed—No Current Verification Items
Open/InProgress/PendingVerification ──author Correct-entry (COR-4)──▶ Reported in Error
Closed—Verified ──added verification required (§8.7) / INV-4──▶ Open (Reopened)
Closed—Administrator ──administrator reopen──▶ Open (Reopened)
Closed—No Current Verification Items ──administrator reopen──▶ Open (Reopened)
Reported in Error ──invalidation of its COR-3/COR-4 chain (INV-7)──▶ Open (Reopened)
```
Any transition not listed is rejected server-side.

### 8.3 Creation, links and suggestions [C] (REQ §4)

- **DEF-1** Reporting a new deficiency shows related existing deficiencies and suggests possible matches; the crew member decides whether to link.
- **DEF-2** Crew members may add and remove links with an audit trail. Links are informational: they do **not** merge responsibilities, transfer assignees, or trigger closure of any other deficiency.
- **DEF-3 [OQ-08 → P]** One deficiency may be supported by **several** failed checks (evidence list). Correcting/invalidating one supporting check never closes the deficiency while another effective failed check or other evidence remains; and per INV-3, invalidation never closes it in any case.

- **DEF-8 [C: owner decision 2026-10-25, OQ-56]** *Severity.* Every deficiency has a **severity level**, chosen by the crew member who reports it from the organization's current levels. Levels are administrator-configured data, not a fixed list: each has a code, a label, a rank (least to most severe) and a **concise criteria description**, kept as numbered revisions (AUD-4). An administrator may add a level, reword or reorder one, or retire it with a new revision; a retired level is not offered, and a deficiency keeps the revision it was reported under. Wherever a reporter chooses a severity, the label and criteria of every offered level are shown beside the choice so the selection can be made accurately. **[P]** The starting levels are: **Low** (cosmetic or minor; does not affect patient care or safe operation; fix at convenience), **Medium** (reduces convenience or a safety margin and a workaround exists; fix in normal scheduling), **High** (impairs equipment or capability needed on a call; fix soon, supervisor should review) and **Critical** (prevents safe patient care or safe driving, or is a safety or regulatory failure; tell a supervisor now). Severity informs people only (OPS-2). A deficiency's severity may be **re-graded** after it is reported by a role holding the §3.3 permission (**[P]** supervisors and administrators): to another current level, with a mandatory explanation, as an append-only deficiency event that keeps the previous and the new level, the actor and the instant, and an audit record (AUD-1). The original grade is never overwritten, and a re-grade does not reassign, extend, close or otherwise change the deficiency.

### 8.4 Deadlines, extensions, age [C] (REQ §4)

- **DEF-4** Each deficiency has an assignee and a deadline, with all revisions retained. The assignee must be an active user who holds the Crew, Supervisor or Supply Officer role [C: owner decision 2026-10-27, [issue #146](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/146)].
- **DEF-5** A crew member may request an extension (reason + proposed date). A supervisor approves, denies, or chooses a different date with explanation. The approved date governs overdue notices. Extensions are unlimited; long-running cases are highlighted.
- **DEF-6** Age since original report is tracked separately from deadline. Administrators configure dashboard age thresholds. There are **no** independent age-based notifications.
- **DEF-7 [C]** Authorized *acknowledgment* may suspend escalation for a configured period, requiring explanation and an action plan; it expires automatically; renewals are audited. Who is authorized: OQ-06/OQ-20.

### 8.5 Awaiting External Dependency [C] (REQ §4)

Mandatory explanation; **one** status/dependency record per deficiency (further detail goes in progress notes); optional follow-up date shown on the dashboard **without** an automatic reminder. Crew members may update the status and details (audit logged) but cannot bypass the deadline or escalation. Each revision of the record is an appended event, never overwritten text. When the dependency ends the deficiency returns to Open or In Progress as the person chooses; if they choose neither, it returns to the status it was in before it began waiting [C: owner decision 2026-10-27, [issue #149](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/149)]. A progress note (§8.1) is a timestamped, attributed, append-only entry on an active deficiency, added by the roles who may update the dependency record (§3.3) [C: owner decision 2026-10-27, [issue #149](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/149)].

### 8.6 Correction and verification [C] (REQ §4)

- **VER-1** Correction may be done by the original crew member or another crew member. The same crew member may both correct and verify.
- **VER-2** A genuine deficiency closes only after **successful verification** or an explicit closure allowed by §8.8. Successful verification closes the linked deficiency automatically.
- **VER-3 [C]** Verification scope: for **kit/compartment** defects, re-check the **entire affected kit/compartment**, resolving current checklist membership and physical kit instances when the correction is recorded, including items added since the report; for **vehicle-level** defects, re-check the **affected systems**, not the whole truck. For a vehicle-level deficiency, affected systems are the affected items recorded when it was reported, plus any supervisor expansion [C: owner decision 2026-10-27, [issue #153](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/153)]. Supervisors may **expand** scope with audit history. For an item-level deficiency the scope is one row for each active physical kit instance of a kit-defined item, or one row for the item requirement (one stock of the item, CHK-11) of an item that is not in a kit (amendment 6.0.0), and each row needs its own qualifying pass. Supervisor expansions are kept with the deficiency: every later correction's scope also includes each expanded item, and each expanded physical kit instance while it still holds that item on the vehicle's current checklist, resolved when the correction is recorded like the rest of the scope [C: owner-approved amendment 1.22.0, [issue #156](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/156)].
- **VER-4 [P]** Scope is materialized as append-only, revisioned rows when the correction is recorded. Each row identifies the item, its checklist definition, the physical kit instance (or no-kit item), and the instant after which a qualifying check must be recorded. A verification check is an ordinary check with `purpose=VERIFICATION` and `verification_for_deficiency_id` set; it counts only for the matching physical kit instance, if any, and only if recorded **after** the correction instant, effective (not invalidated/superseded to a non-Pass), and Passed. When every scope row is satisfied, the deficiency transitions to Closed—Verified in the same transaction (idempotent). A verification check is accepted only for a deficiency on the check's vehicle that is Pending Verification, and only for an item and kit instance in the newest scope revision; it is Passed or Failed (Unable to Inspect does not verify). A Pass in an older revision never satisfies a newer one, except across a supervisor expansion: an expansion appends a new revision that repeats every row of the revision it expands with that row's instant and adds the new rows with the expansion instant as their instant, and an effective Pass that satisfied a repeated row still satisfies it. Passes never carry over to the revision of a new correction [C: owner-approved amendment 1.22.0, [issue #156](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/156)]. A Failed verification check is evidence for the deficiency it verifies and for no other (VER-5).
- **VER-5 [C: owner decision 2026-10-27]** A verification attempt that Fails does not close the deficiency. It records new evidence (§8.3) and returns the deficiency from Pending Verification to In Progress. The recorded correction and its verification scope remain in the history; a later correction creates a new correction event and a new scope revision.
- **VER-6 [P]** Ordinary routine checks can double as verification only if recorded with `purpose=VERIFICATION` against that deficiency; this prevents accidental closure and honors "no false pass".

### 8.7 Added verification after closure [C] (REQ §4)

If a supervisor adds required verification after closure, the deficiency **reopens automatically** with history preserved; only its **current assignee** is notified (previous assignee is retained by default unless reassigned). The supervisor must set a **new** resolution deadline; the former deadline stays in history. The supervisor names the items (and physical kit instances) to add; they are recorded as a supervisor expansion and are verified after the next correction [C: owner-approved amendment 1.22.0, [issue #156](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/156)].

### 8.8 Explicit closure without verification [C]

General closure of a deficiency without verification is **Administrator-only** and always an explicit act. **[OQ-34 → P]** An explanation is mandatory; the closing administrator is recorded as the actor; closure type is distinguishable from verified closure in all reports.

**[C: owner-approved amendment 1.19.0 (2026-10-30, [issue #153](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/153))] Empty current scope.** Checklist publication or kit-assignment changes can remove every item from a deficiency's correction-time verification scope after it was reported. Before a correction has materialized a verification scope, correction is refused with `EMPTY_SCOPE` and records no correction event; the system does not fall back to report-time items. A Supervisor or Administrator may instead explicitly close the deficiency through a distinct, attributed empty-scope closure event, with a mandatory explanation and the status Closed — No Current Verification Items. The command re-resolves scope under the checklist and vehicle locks and refuses if any current verification item remains. A deficiency already in Pending Verification keeps its materialized scope and does not use this exception. The existing Administrator-only closure of any active deficiency remains unchanged. Only an Administrator may reopen either explicit-closure status.

### 8.9 Known-deficiency check suspension [C] (REQ §4)

- **SUS-1** A supervisor may suspend *specific* repetitive routine inspection checks for a known defect, explicitly choosing which checks. Display: "Known Problem — Check Suspended".
- **SUS-2** Suspension is **not** a Pass, affects only the explicitly suspended checks, and never changes operational status. For an item outside a kit, a suspension applies to one item requirement (one stock of the item) only (amendment 6.0.0). Unaffected checks remain required (e.g. continue counting the remaining masks).
- **SUS-3** Suspension **ends automatically** when verification closes the deficiency.
- **SUS-4 [P]** If the deficiency is closed by administrator instead of verification, or is Reported in Error, suspension also ends (a suspension with no open deficiency is invalid); the closing event lists the suspensions ended. Suspended obligations are shown as "Suspended", never "Satisfied".
- **SUS-5 [C: owner-approved 2026-11-03, [issue #32](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/32)]** **Ending a suspension early.** While the deficiency is still open, a Supervisor or Administrator (the roles that may suspend, §3.3) may end one suspension at a time: one suspended item, with its physical kit instance for a kit-defined item. An explanation is optional. The deficiency's status and its other suspensions are unchanged. The end is an attributed, append-only deficiency event that names the suspension it ends; it sends no notification. From the end instant the item's routine checks are required again under the normal schedule: an obligation whose window closed while it was suspended stays "Suspended" (no backfill), and one whose window is still open is required again with its normal due time. No extra inspection is created; a supervisor may still require one (ADH-1). An ended suspension cannot be ended again or resumed; suspending the check again needs a new suspension (SUS-1).

## 9. Operational status

- **OPS-1 [C]** Statuses: **In Service**, **Limited Service**, **Out of Service**. Only supervisors/administrators set status, **manually**, with a required explanation, history, and notification. Crew members may *request* a change for supervisor decision.
- **OPS-2 [C]** Severity, deficiency events, expired certifications, failed checks and regulatory overdue status **never** change status automatically. The system may raise alerts and recommendations only.
- **OPS-3 [OQ-21 → P]** Limited-service categories (e.g. which functions are restricted) are undefined; R1 records limited service as free-text restrictions plus the mandatory explanation. Who may approve crew change requests: supervisor (as above) pending OQ-21.
- **OPS-4 [P]** A status-change request is a record with requester, reason, target status, decision, decider and decision explanation; denial requires an explanation.
- **OPS-5 [C]** While Out of Service, routine obligations are exempt (§5.9).
- **OPS-6 [P]** The status is an audited, append-only event stream; "current status" is the latest event.

## 10. Kits, inventory and expirations (primarily R2; minimum sealed-kit workflow in R1, §10.1)

- **STK-A [C]** Physical kits, bags and containers are tracked individually with identifiers, location/vehicle assignment, contents, seals, and full movement history (REQ §3).
- **STK-B [C]** An **intact valid seal** may substitute for inspecting contents until the **earliest contained expiration**. A broken or expired seal requires a full contents check. Seal breaks/reseals are recorded events with actor and time. **[P]** The substitution is a rule in the obligation engine (a `SEAL_SUBSTITUTION` rule type, subject to §5.6's closed-rule policy) and records which seal was relied upon.
- **STK-C [OQ-14]** Periodic/random full-content audits of sealed items are desired; tentative weekly cadence is **not decided** (frequency, random vs scheduled, reseal handling, exemptions). R1 implements only a **manual, supervisor-requested** full audit (SEAL-7); scheduled or random selection is not implemented in R1.
- **STK-D [C]** Quantities have **exact** configured targets; both too few **and too many** are flagged, with immediate correction available in the workflow.
- **STK-E [C]** Item expirations with configurable advance alerts. **No lot-number tracking.**
- **STK-F [C]** Station inventory: storage positions (room/cabinet/shelf), transfers, restocking ambulances with quantity deductions and attribution, receipts (quantity/location/expiration), low-stock alerts and reorder thresholds.
- **STK-G [C]** Generic supply catalog (no mandatory branded SKU); purchasing may choose equivalent products and record multiple vendors/prices without an equivalence-approval workflow; consistent units of measure across order, receipt, store and stock.
- **STK-H [C]** Purchase orders: vendors, pricing, history, partial receipts, outstanding quantities, completion.
- **STK-I [OQ-22]** Starting storage positions, catalog units, PO workflow and vendor fields are undefined.

### 10.1 R1 minimum sealed-kit workflow [C/P] (owner decisions, [issue #18](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/18), 2026-10-20)

Prototype reference: `prototype/sealed-kit.html` ([PR #29](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/pull/29)), non-normative.

- **SEAL-1 [C]** R1 includes, for each sealed kit, its identity (`kit_instance`), listed contents with exact expected quantities and expirations (STK-D), and attributed seal events (`kit_seal_event`). Full movement workflows (transfers between stations) and scheduled audits remain R2; R1 keeps only the kit assignment record that TIME-1a needs (§18). The actual list of sealed kits and contents is owner-supplied (OQ-01). A kit instance is sealed only when a Supervisor or Administrator designates it sealable (§3.3; owner decision 2026-11-12, [issue #131](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/131)). The designation is append-only history, effective when recorded, and a kit never designated is not sealed; a kit that is not sealed is checked by its contents. On the outage paper checklist (§17.8 DEP-11) a sealed kit prints a seal-or-contents row and any other kit a contents-checked row.
- **SEAL-2 [C]** Physical seals have **no unique serial**. The crew member who seals a kit handwrites their crew ID and the earliest expiration among its expiration-tracked contents on the seal; when none of its contents has a tracked expiration, they write “no expiration” instead. Repeated crew IDs are valid. A seal event is identified by the kit plus its attributed event sequence. An optional seal-identifier field may be added later for prelabeled seals; it is not required in R1.
- **SEAL-3 [C]** Every item listed as inside the kit is covered by the seal; there is no hidden exception. Equipment that needs independent checks is modeled outside the sealed kit.
- **SEAL-4 [C]** **Verification.** An intact, unexpired seal that is not subject to a pending audit, and whose listed items have no open deficiency (OQ-47, decided), may substitute for inspecting the contents (STK-B). The crew member enters the crew ID read from the physical seal (it must match the seal on file) and attests that the seal is intact. The system records a `SEAL_SUBSTITUTION` event naming the seal event relied upon. It creates **no individual Passed check records** (PRN-1 is unchanged), does not close or alter a deficiency, and does not bypass a supervisor suspension. While a listed item has an open deficiency the seal cannot substitute and the kit needs a full inspection; the deficiency itself is handled only through §8.
- **SEAL-5 [C]** **Break.** Any authorized member may break a seal; a reason is required. The event records actor, server instant, the seal marking and the reason. While the kit is open, or broken, the seal cannot substitute and the contents must be inspected.
- **SEAL-6 [C]** **Full inspection and reseal.** The crew member inspects every listed item according to its configured kind, enters a fresh count for every quantity item and a fresh expiration date for every expiration-tracked item; prior certified values are never prefilled. Quantities that differ from the exact target are recorded as deficiencies (STK-D) and do not block resealing. An item whose expiration date is before today (i.e. already expired; an item is still usable on its expiration date, OQ-46) cannot be accepted into a new seal; it must be replaced first. The system computes and displays the earliest tracked expiration to write on the seal, or “no expiration” when no listed item has a tracked expiration. The crew member enters the crew ID written on the new seal and attests that they inspected every item and wrote the ID and the tracked expiration or “no expiration”. A seal applied while any listed item still has an open deficiency (of any kind, OQ-47) does not substitute for contents, and sealing never silently closes a deficiency.
- **SEAL-7 [C]** **Full-content audit.** Only a Supervisor (or Administrator) may request one, for now; the owner intends this to become permission-based later (§3.3). While an audit is pending the seal cannot substitute even if intact. It is satisfied only by a break, a full inspection of every item and a reseal, which records an "audit fulfilled" event. Automatic or random selection and cadence remain undecided (OQ-14, STK-C).
- **SEAL-8 [C]** **Expiration.** Once the earliest contained expiration date has passed (the day after it), the seal is no longer valid and a full inspection and reseal are required. An item may be used on its expiration date but not after it (OQ-46, decided). Early "expiring soon" warnings and reorder flags are deferred to a future release (supply and inventory, STK-E and STK-F): replacement stock may already be on hand, and reorder prompts belong with stock levels. A seal marked ‘no expiration’ has no expiration-based invalidation; break and pending audit still prevent substitution.
- **SEAL-9 [P]** Seal events are append-only and attributed (AUD-1, AUD-3), carry operation ids for idempotent replay (REC-4), and are serialized per kit so that two concurrent breaks or reseals cannot both succeed; the loser receives a conflict result. Safe default, reversible.

- **SEAL-10 [C]** (OQ-53) Seal handling is per kit instance. Only a valid `SEAL_SUBSTITUTION` for that instance (SEAL-4) or the full inspection of that instance's listed items (SEAL-5, SEAL-6) satisfies the instance's obligation for the kit's listed contents. Breaking a seal satisfies nothing: it only means the contents must be inspected (SEAL-5). A verification, break, inspection or reseal of one instance never stands in for another instance of the same kit type, and a vehicle with no instance has none required.

- **STK-J [P]** Stock quantities are derived from an append-only **stock movement ledger** (receipt, transfer, restock, adjustment, count) rather than a mutable counter; adjustments require an explanation.

### 10.2 Sealable cabinets [C] (owner decisions, [issue #131](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/131), 2026-11-12)

- **CAB-1 [C] Designation.** A vehicle compartment may be designated **sealable**. The designation belongs to the compartment's revision, so it is effective-dated and history shows what was sealable at any instant. Only a Supervisor or Administrator may change it (§3.3), by publishing a new compartment revision with an effective date-time. Compartments are not sealable unless designated. Vehicle systems are never sealable.
- **CAB-2 [C] What a cabinet seal covers.** An intact cabinet seal covers everything placed in that compartment at that instant: every checklist item located in the compartment, and every kit instance assigned to it with that kit's listed contents. As with kits (SEAL-3), there is no hidden exception; equipment that needs its own checks is placed outside the sealable compartment. An item with null placement is not located in a compartment and is never covered by a cabinet seal; it appears in the Not placed group under DEP-11 (amendment 5.0.0). A position referenced by a cabinet seal is the vehicle-local compartment identity.
- **CAB-3 [C] Marking.** As for kits (SEAL-2), the crew member handwrites their crew ID and the earliest tracked expiration among everything the seal covers, including tracked expirations of kits inside it. If nothing covered has a tracked expiration, the marking says ‘no expiration’. Seals have no serial number.
- **CAB-4 [C] Verification.** An intact, unexpired cabinet seal may substitute for checking everything it covers, under the same conditions as SEAL-4: the seal is not subject to a pending audit, no covered item or kit has an open deficiency, and no supervisor suspension applies. The crew member enters the crew ID read from the seal (it must match the seal on file) and attests that the seal is intact. The system records a cabinet seal substitution naming the seal event relied on. It creates no individual Passed check records (PRN-1 unchanged) and never closes or alters a deficiency. An eligible cabinet seal may also substitute for the kit-presence obligations of the kit-list lines for this cabinet. At full inspection before reseal, the inspector records the actual count against each applicable line. The reseal retains the line and count it certified. A mismatch opens a deficiency under KDEF-10 and blocks subsequent substitution while that deficiency remains open. A change in the applicable line or in the assignment of a covered kit after reseal invalidates reliance on the seal until the cabinet is inspected and resealed again. A substitution links explicitly to each eligible presence obligation and does not fabricate a count check.
- **CAB-5 [C] Break, full inspection and reseal.** These follow SEAL-5 and SEAL-6 for everything the seal covers. **Break:** any authorized member may break the seal, giving a reason. **Full inspection:** every covered loose item is then inspected, and every covered kit is handled under §10.1; a kit inside with its own intact, valid seal may still be verified by that seal (OQ-65). **Reseal:** this records the new crew ID and the earliest tracked expiration, or ‘no expiration’ when nothing covered has a tracked expiration; an expired item cannot be accepted into a new seal. The cabinet's full inspection includes a fresh, individually attributed kit-presence count for every published kit-list line in the cabinet, including lines whose definitions no assigned vehicle checklist inspects (KDEF-10 and KDEF-12). The inspection records the exact effective check used for each line; it must have been recorded by the inspecting crew member after this inspection began, remain effective at reseal, and name that same line. A mismatch opens a deficiency and does not prevent resealing; the resulting seal cannot substitute while that deficiency remains open (SEAL-4 and SEAL-6).
- **CAB-6 [C] Audit and expiry.** SEAL-7 (supervisor-requested full audit) and SEAL-8 (the seal stops counting the day after the earliest covered expiration) apply to cabinet seals.
- **CAB-7 [C] Designation changes.** Sealable to not sealable: from the new revision's effective instant, any cabinet seal stops substituting, and the compartment's contents are checked individually. Not sealable to sealable: nothing is substituted until the cabinet is inspected in full and sealed.
- **CAB-8 [C] Per vehicle and per instance.** Seal handling is per vehicle compartment. A seal on one vehicle's cabinet never stands in for another vehicle's cabinet. SEAL-9 (append-only, attributed, idempotent and serialized seal events) applies to cabinet seals.
- **CAB-9 [C] Paper fallback.** On the outage paper checklist (§17.8 DEP-11), a sealable compartment prints one seal-or-contents row instead of its items, in the same form as a kit row: seal intact, with the crew ID and earliest expiration from the seal, or contents checked, with exceptions.

## 11. Maintenance, assets and contracts (primarily R3)

- **MNT-1 [C]** Track individual equipment assets with serial numbers, current assignment, transfers, maintenance and repairs, linked to deficiencies where appropriate (REQ §5).
- **MNT-2 [C]** Preventive maintenance rules are calendar- or usage-based, **whichever occurs first**. Usage is **manually entered**; vehicles support mileage and engine hours.
- **MNT-3 [C]** Record vendors/contacts, parts/labor/total cost, invoices, service reports, and work history.
- **MNT-4 [C]** Warranties/service contracts: provider, covered vehicles/assets (multiple), coverage, term/renewals, costs, attached documents, expiration alerts.
- **MNT-5 [P]** Preventive-maintenance due state follows the same "derived, never stored as authoritative" rule as inspections (§19) and never changes operational status (OPS-2).
- **MNT-6 [OQ-18]** Future engine-hour mandatory triggers and counter validation are open.

## 12. Regulatory and historical compliance (R3–R4)

- **REG-1 [C]** Administrators define recurring regulatory inspections/certifications by vehicle/equipment type, distinct from preventive maintenance (REQ §6).
- **REG-2 [C]** Store requirement version, results, effective dates, due dates, documents, reminders and history. Overdue items appear on the dashboard but **never** automatically remove a vehicle from service.
- **REG-3 [C]** Record only **externally authorized** legal extensions/exemptions, naming the issuing authority, evidence, and revised deadline. An administrator cannot create a legal exemption by fiat; **[P]** the entry cannot be saved without authority name and an attached evidence reference.
- **REG-4 [C]** The system produces **auditable "as of" views** reproducing then-applicable rules, inspections and exemptions — not merely current status.
- **REG-5 [OQ-19]** Actual regulatory requirements, authorities and proof rules are not defined; no regulatory content is asserted by this spec.
- **REG-6 [P]** As-of reconstruction is possible because requirement versions, obligation determinations, checks, corrections, invalidations and exemptions are append-only with effective instants (§19.3). The as-of query takes a timestamp `T` and ignores events with `server_recorded_at > T`; the report states that invalidations recorded after `T` are, by default, **shown as of `T` (valid)** with an optional "include later corrections" mode. **Owner confirmation:** OQ-39.

## 13. Notifications [C / OQ-20]

- **NOT-1 [C]** Channels: email and in-app. Administrators control which events/channels are mandatory; optional preferences are delegated to users; recipients are designated by roles and/or users and **de-duplicated** per event.
- **NOT-2 [C]** Examples with stated recipients: deficiency reopened by added verification → current assignee only (§8.7); status changes → notification (§9); expiring stocking deviation, expiring contracts/certifications, overdue deadlines → alerts.
- **NOT-3 [OQ-20]** The mandatory event list, escalation timings, role-recipient mappings, email safety rules and age-highlight thresholds are undecided. **[P]** R1 ships a minimal configurable set: deficiency assigned, reopened, extension requested/decided, deadline overdue, status changed/requested, session reopened by someone else, deficiency admin-closed, check due on a vehicle (to that vehicle's current crew, ASG-7), a vehicle assignment ended or changed by someone else (to that person, ASG-2), and a crew member moved off a vehicle (to that vehicle's remaining crew, ASG-2). All are configurable and none are hard-coded.
- **NOT-4 [P]** Email bodies contain **no** patient data and minimal operational detail (a deep link rather than full content). Delivery is attempted via a durable outbox with idempotent per-recipient delivery records; failures are visible to administrators.
- **NOT-5 [P]** A notification never mutates operational data and cannot itself be the only record of an event.

## 14. Reporting, search and exports

- **RPT-1 [C]** Search/filter completed and incomplete inspections, checks, deficiencies, and corrections (REQ §6).
- **RPT-2 [C]** Export CSV and Excel. **[P]** exports are role-checked, audit-logged (who, filter, row count, time), and size-bounded with explicit truncation messages; CSV output neutralizes spreadsheet-formula injection by prefixing risky leading characters in text cells.
- **RPT-3 [C]** Saved/shareable filter definitions with role-controlled sharing.
- **RPT-4 [C]** Participation reports: counts, dates, inspected items/categories, vehicle, recency, with filters. **No quotas or required minimums** anywhere in the product.
- **RPT-5 [C]** Fleet dashboard: service status, due/overdue items, inspection progress, deficiencies, compliance, readiness. Suspended items and deferred external dependencies remain visible in supervisor contexts (§8.5, §8.9).
- **RPT-5a [C: owner-decided 2026-11-08/09, issue #66; amended 2026-11-10, issue #80]** *Fleet-readiness summary counts.* In the current fleet-readiness summary: (a) **Overdue.** Once an item has a later effective check, an earlier obligation of that same item (for an item inside a kit, the same physical kit instance; OQ-63) that is still unsatisfied after its grace deadline `G` is no longer counted as Overdue, whether or not its late limit `L` (SCH-2) has passed; it is not retained in the Overdue count for the rest of the look-back. An obligation still unsatisfied after `L` stays permanently Overdue (missed) in history and reports (SCH-2), and this rule never overrides the later check's own outcome (for example, a Fail still leaves the item not ready, §5.4). The later effective check must meet the linked later-obligation rule in OQ-63, including its treatment of retirement. (b) **Missed.** The summary shows a separate **Missed** count: obligations in the readiness look-back that are unsatisfied after `L` at the read instant. A miss stays in the Missed count after the item is re-checked until its due instant D is more than seven days before the read instant. An obligation due exactly seven days before the read instant remains included if it is otherwise eligible for Missed. (c) **Placement.** The Missed count appears on the readiness page per vehicle and in the fleet totals, in the readiness API, and as a summarized count in Reports. (d) **As-of.** The owner decided that the default as-of **Missed** count follows the record as known at that moment: it counts obligations already unsatisfied after `L` at that instant whose due instant D is no more than seven days before that instant, including exactly seven days, using only events with `server_recorded_at` at or before it, so a correction, invalidation or supersession recorded later does not change it, while the current summary reflects all corrections. The as-of **Overdue** count follows the existing rule of §19.3 and REG-6 (the same functions over events recorded at or before the instant); this restates the existing rule and is not a new owner decision. The optional "include later corrections" mode of REG-6 is not part of this requirement (OQ-62). Any stored summary of these counts is a projection (§17.4): rebuildable from events and verified against recomputation (§19).
- **RPT-6 [C]** Optional automated report generation and email delivery; not required for initial release. **[OQ-16]** templates, who may schedule/receive, formats and retention are undefined.
- **RPT-7 [P]** Reports distinguish: Passed, Not Applicable (rule), Carried over (rule), Suspended, Exempt (out of service), Unable, Failed, Invalidated, Superseded, and Completed late — never collapsing them into a single "compliant" flag.

## 15. Integrations and identity

### 15.1 Google Workspace sign-in [C / OQ-13]

- **AUTH-1 [C]** Sign-in uses **organization-managed Google Workspace identities only** — not personal Google accounts and not merely any account whose address claims the organization's domain (REQ §7).
- **AUTH-2 [C]** Disabling Workspace access must revoke entry while retaining historical attribution.
- **AUTH-3 [P]** Identity key is the provider's stable subject identifier, **not** the email address. Email, display name and domain are attributes captured at sign-in. A sign-in is accepted only if the server has validated the identity token's signature, issuer, audience, expiry, and that the account belongs to the configured Workspace organization **as asserted by a verified claim** (domain hint alone is insufficient; the specific verification mechanism must be confirmed against Google's current documentation before implementation and is therefore OQ-13). Until verified, no production authentication.
- **AUTH-4 [P]** Revocation: sessions are short-lived and re-validated; a disabled user's active sessions are refused within a configurable bound (the mechanism for detecting Workspace disablement must be confirmed from official documentation — OQ-13). Deprovisioned users remain as inactive `users` rows so attribution stays intact.
- **AUTH-5 [OQ-13]** Emergency ("break-glass") access if Google sign-in is unavailable is undecided. **[P]** Safe default: no alternate password login; documented operator procedure only.
- **AUTH-6 [P]** New valid users are auto-provisioned with Crew only (§3.1), after the identity check passes.

### 15.2 Humanity roster (schedule feed) [C / OQ-17]

- **HUM-1 [C]** Humanity/ShiftPlanning is the **schedule source** only (not payroll/timeclock). The schedule is refreshed/cached about **hourly** (REQ §8).
- **HUM-2 [OQ-17]** API documentation, credentials, authentication, shift/user/ambulance mapping, refresh/backoff behavior and stale/unavailable behavior are undefined. This spec defines **no** Humanity API call; **[P]** it defines an internal `RosterProvider` interface returning `(person, vehicle_assignment, shift_start, shift_end, fetched_at)` so the adapter can be implemented later and stubbed in tests.
- **HUM-3 [P]** Staleness: if `now − fetched_at` exceeds a configured limit (default proposal: 3 h, 3× the refresh), roster data is treated as **unavailable** for conditional rules (CND-3). Refresh uses bounded retries with backoff; it never loops unbounded. The limit governs new determinations; re-evaluating an existing waiver follows CND-7, under which aged but unchanged data does not reinstate it.
- **HUM-4 [C]** Credentials are never committed; they come from deployment secrets (§17.6).
- **HUM-5 [C]** The roster does **not** restrict who may perform equipment checks. Volunteers have no timeclock; paid staff's payroll timeclock is out of scope.

### 15.3 Actual-roster correction [C] (REQ §8)

At inspection time a crew member may confirm/correct the **actual roster for that inspection only**. The system retains both the scheduled and corrected rosters and who changed it. There is **no write-back** to Humanity and no effect on other sessions' own records. **[C: owner-approved 2026-10-15, OQ-44]** Because several sessions may coexist for one vehicle yet an obligation's waiver is shared: the roster governing a conditional determination is the scheduled roster overlaid with the **most recent** (by server time) actual-roster correction among sessions on that vehicle covering the determination instant. Every correction is retained and triggers a re-evaluation event; if two corrections from different sessions disagree on membership, supervisors are notified and the determination records both and fails closed (the obligation is required) until a supervisor resolves it. Each determination records the roster provenance (scheduled vs which correction).

### 15.4 Vehicle crew assignment [C / P] ([issue #202](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/202), OQ-78)

A vehicle crew assignment records that a person is working on a vehicle from one instant until an end time. The app uses it to show each person their own vehicle first and to send vehicle notices to that vehicle's crew. It is separate from the scheduled roster (§15.2) and from an inspection's actual-roster correction (§15.3). Shift times vary between agencies and between people, so the system defines no shift or day of its own: each assignment carries its own end time.

- **ASG-1 [C]** *Source.* Assignments are made in the app. Once the Humanity roster is connected (HUM-2, OQ-17), a roster entry that names a vehicle pre-fills an assignment with that entry's start and end times, recorded with source `ROSTER`. A later manual change to that assignment wins, and a roster refresh does not override it. Until the adapter exists, every assignment is manual.
- **ASG-2 [C]** *Who may change it.* The server checks every change (§3.2).
  - (a) Any active user with a role may assign themselves to a vehicle, move themselves to another vehicle, and extend or end their own assignment.
  - (b) Anyone currently assigned to a vehicle may extend or end another person's assignment on that vehicle. They may also add any person to that vehicle, including moving someone who is currently on another vehicle (ASG-3 ends the other assignment in the same event). This lets a partner fix a wrong-truck assignment without a Supervisor.
  - (c) A Supervisor or Administrator may assign, move, extend or end anyone's assignment.

  When someone else ends or changes a person's assignment under (b) or (c), that person is notified. When a person is moved off a vehicle, that vehicle's remaining crew are notified too, for example "Alex moved Sam from Medic 4 to Medic 7" (NOT-3).

  When people are being added to a vehicle, the app never hides anyone who can be added. A person already on another vehicle is listed with that vehicle and their end time, for example "on Medic 4 until 18:00", and adding them is shown as a move.
- **ASG-3 [C]** *One vehicle per person.* A person is assigned to at most one vehicle at a time. Assigning them to a second vehicle ends the first assignment in the same recorded event, so nobody is ever on two vehicles. A vehicle may have any number of people assigned. Only an active vehicle (CFG-1) can be assigned. Deactivating a vehicle ends its current assignments, and the deactivation records that it did.
- **ASG-4 [C]** *Hours.*
  - An assignment starts when it is recorded. Advance or backdated starts are not part of this amendment.
  - Whoever makes the assignment enters its end time every time. The system keeps no default or "usual" hours.
  - The end time must be after the start and no more than the maximum assignment length after it. The maximum is an Administrator setting with a default of 24 hours (OQ-79).
  - An assignment stops applying at its end time, or earlier when it is ended. Its expiry needs no event.
- **ASG-5 [C]** *Never carried forward; easy to extend.* The system never extends or renews an assignment by itself. An Extend action sets a later end time, again no more than the maximum length after the moment of extending. It is open to the person and to whoever else ASG-2 allows.
  - From 30 minutes before the end time, the person's Home offers a one-step "Still on [vehicle]? Extend".
  - After the end time, the same prompt offers to assign them to that vehicle again, as a new assignment.
- **ASG-6 [C]** *Checks are never restricted.* An assignment never limits who may open an inspection, record a check or report a problem (§3.3, HUM-5). It is not the effective roster for conditional obligations: CND-2 still uses the scheduled roster with §15.3 corrections, and an assignment never waives or reinstates an obligation.
- **ASG-7 [C]** *First uses.*
  - Home lists the signed-in person's current vehicle first, then the rest, and shows every vehicle as today when there is no current assignment.
  - For vehicle notices, "the vehicle's current crew" is an available recipient designation under NOT-1. It is resolved at the moment the event is recorded, and duplicates are removed per event. The first events to use it are a problem reported or reopened on the vehicle and a check due on the vehicle (NOT-3). Administrators still control which events and channels apply (NOT-1).
  - Other workflows may use assignments only after a further amendment.
- **ASG-8 [P]** *History and visibility.*
  - Assignments are kept as an append-only event history (AUD-1). Each event is `ASSIGN`, `MOVE`, `EXTEND` or `END` and records the person, the vehicle, the end time, the actor, the server instant, the source (`SELF`, `CREW`, `SUPERVISOR`, or later `ROSTER`) and, for `END`, whether it was ended early.
  - "Who is on which vehicle now" is a projection rebuilt from these events and the clock (§17.4, §19).
  - Every active user with a role can see each active vehicle's current crew: names and end times.
  - Supervisors and Administrators can see the history. The audit log records every change (§16.1).

### 17.3 Live synchronization [P]

State-changing commits publish an event after the transaction commits; subscribers get per-vehicle updates. Clients treat pushes as hints and reconcile from the authoritative query on (re)connect, so missed messages cannot leave a permanently wrong view. Another viewer of the same vehicle sees a committed change within 5 seconds under normal conditions, typically within 1–2 seconds. The supervisors' fleet-readiness screen reflects a committed change within 30 seconds under normal conditions, typically within 10–15 seconds. A client may combine several pushes into one re-read and delay it by up to 2 seconds. The person who recorded the change sees the server-acknowledged result at once (REC-2) and does not need a full re-read of the vehicle's checklist to see it. Pushes remain hints: the client still reconciles from the authoritative query when the connection is (re)established. [C: owner-decided 2026-11-07, OQ-59; remaining real-time UI details stay with OQ-15]

### 17.4 Derived state [P]

Current statuses (obligation state, item attention, deficiency status, vehicle readiness) are **projections** computed from authoritative event rows, rules and effective dates. Projections may be materialized for speed but must be rebuildable from events; a verification routine compares projections to a recomputation (§19).

### 17.7 Design rules adopted from the spike for Django + MariaDB [P]

Adopted as starting rules for the M1 contracts and audit issues ([issue #20](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/20), [issue #23](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/23)); the contracts issue may refine them with evidence, through review. Evidence: spike report §3 and §5.

1. **Two database accounts:** a schema-owner account used only for migrations, and a least-privilege runtime account with `SELECT, INSERT` on append-only tables and no DDL. Django models over append-only tables are **unmanaged** or guarded so `save()`/`update()`/`delete()` cannot be used on them; the Django admin must not expose them for editing.
2. **Idempotent writes** use the operation id's unique key: attempt the INSERT, catch the duplicate-key error, return the original record. **Never `INSERT IGNORE`** (it downgrades other errors and defeats strict mode).
3. **Per-(vehicle, item) lock rows** are created when a checklist version is published and locked with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` inside `transaction.atomic()`; the runtime account holds `UPDATE` on that lock table only. Lock rows are not created lazily inside the check transaction (observed deadlocks). Wrap the check write in a bounded retry on deadlock only.
4. **Every connection** sets UTC (`time_zone`) and strict SQL mode explicitly (Django's default session time zone was `SYSTEM` and it does not add strictness), and a startup check refuses to run otherwise. CHECK constraints and triggers back up the application checks.
5. **Sessions** use signed cookies or a store the runtime account can write; cookies are `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Strict`; CSRF protection stays on (Origin/Referer checking included).
6. **Tokens:** validate with a pinned algorithm and required `exp`, `iat`, `iss`, `aud`, `sub`; Google's organization-claim semantics must be confirmed from Google's documentation before sign-in is built ([issue #25](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/25) adds a real-Workspace verification).
7. **Live updates** use WebSockets (Django Channels is the spike-tested option). A shared channel layer is needed once more than one worker runs; choosing it is part of the deployment decisions, not decided here. **Every WebSocket connection must be authenticated and authorized per vehicle before it joins a group, and must be rejected otherwise**; the spike's WebSocket consumer is deliberately unauthenticated and must not be reused as written (the Perl probe checked the session, the Django probe did not).
8. **Admin separation:** the database administrator account is not used by the application, its use is rare and logged outside the database; shipping audit events to a second store is to be considered in the audit-log issue.

### 17.8 Deployment, secrets and recovery [OQ-24; [issue #16](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/16)]

Owner decisions recorded on [issue #16](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/16) on 2026-10-21 ([decision record](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance-archive/issues/16#issuecomment-6322809972)). The owner then approved the items first proposed as **[P]** on that issue, so every item below is confirmed. Operational details (retention counts, check cadence, alert thresholds) are starting values the operator may tune within the stated recovery objectives.

- **DEP-1 [C]** Production runs on one Linux VPS operated for the organization.
- **DEP-2 [C]** One application process is assumed initially. A multi-process deployment needs a shared channel layer (§17.7 item 7), which is then a new decision.
- **DEP-3 [C]** The organization names an accountable operator and a deputy before the pilot. The names are held by the owner and are not committed to the repository (§0.3). Their roles may be filled by volunteers, paid staff, or a mix, according to the agency's staffing model.
- **DEP-4 [C]** Planning budget: about USD 25 per month for the server, off-host backup storage and monitoring together. This is a planning estimate, not a hard ceiling, and donated resources may offset part of the cost. If real prices differ materially, the owner decides. The planning estimate assumes no paid contractor and no warm spare; personnel may be volunteers, paid staff, or a mix, according to the agency's staffing model.
- **DEP-5 [C]** Binary logging stays enabled (point-in-time recovery, ARC-7). The production server configuration sets `log_bin_trust_function_creators=1` so that the schema-owner account, which has no SUPER privilege, can run the trigger migrations. On the pinned MariaDB 10.11 image, without that setting `CREATE TRIGGER` fails with error 1419 while binary logging is on, and of the narrower privileges tried (`BINLOG ADMIN`, `SET USER`, `BINLOG REPLAY`) none lifted it; only SUPER did. The same setting is required on MariaDB 11.8; the 11.8 rehearsal ran the trigger migrations with it, and the restore rehearsal confirms it on the version production uses. The runtime account never holds SUPER or trigger-creation powers (§17.7 item 1). The first restore rehearsal confirms the setting against the MariaDB documentation and that a rebuilt server carries it. A trigger records its creating account as DEFINER: rotate that account's password, never drop or rename it, and verify definers after any restore or account change (a missing definer makes every insert into a table with an INSERT trigger fail, error 1449).
- **DEP-6 [C]** Recovery objectives: RPO 15 minutes and RTO 24 hours. Mechanism: a nightly encrypted full backup plus binary-log shipping off the host.
- **DEP-7 [C]** The encrypted off-host copy lives in an account separate from the server, held by an authorized recovery custodian. The operator holds a write-only key. Restores are performed by authorized agency personnel, and a production restore needs the owner's authorization. Provider snapshots alone do not satisfy this.
- **DEP-8 [C]** Backup retention: 30 daily and 12 monthly full backups, plus binary logs back to the oldest retained full backup. This is distinct from record retention (AUD-5, OQ-35): application data is never pruned because backups age out, and backups carry the same access controls as production.
- **DEP-9 [C]** A restore rehearsal each quarter into a throwaway environment, including the projection rebuild comparison (AC-26). A rehearsal copy contains real data and is handled and destroyed under production rules, never on a developer machine or in CI.
- **DEP-10 [C]** Exposure and operations defaults: a reverse proxy terminates TLS with automatic certificate renewal and forwards to the application on loopback or a Unix socket; the database listens on loopback only; the host firewall admits only HTTPS and restricted SSH, plus HTTP on port 80 solely for ACME HTTP-01 certificate challenges: on port 80 the proxy answers only `/.well-known/acme-challenge/` and permanently redirects every other request to HTTPS, never serving application content over HTTP (DNS-01 with narrowly scoped DNS credentials remains an alternative that keeps port 80 closed); sign-in and exports are rate-limited at the proxy as well as in the application (§16.2); secrets live in a root-owned mode-0600 file outside the repository and web root, rotated yearly, on operator change and on suspected exposure, with one exception: the outgoing-mail relay password, when the relay uses one, is stored by the application encrypted with a key held in that file, is never displayed back, and can be replaced only by an Administrator (DEP-13); alerts cover site unreachable, certificate expiry within 14 days, disk space, a backup or log-shipping run older than expected, the database refusing connections, and repeated 5xx errors.
- **DEP-11 [C]** Outage plan (NFR-5): printed checklists for each vehicle and kit type are kept in each rig, outside the app. Until a back-entry feature exists (OQ-50), paper records stay outside the app and are not entered as ordinary live checks. On a printed vehicle checklist, unplaced items print in a clearly labeled **Not placed** section, with their expected check data, and are not omitted from the paper fallback; the cabinet-seal row never covers items in that section (CAB-2; amendment 5.0.0).
- **DEP-12 [C]** Pilot gates: the operator and deputy are named; one restore rehearsal has passed (DEP-9, including a restore to a chosen timestamp and the restore time at a realistic data size, two items the spike left unverified); and the trigger setting (DEP-5) is confirmed on the production host; and each DEP-13 alert has been induced on a throwaway server and its delivery to both recipients confirmed.
- **DEP-13 [C] Operations alerts and the outgoing-mail relay** (owner decisions 2026-11-16, [issue #8](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/8)).
  - **The relay.** An Administrator configures the organization's outgoing-mail relay in the app (§3.3 "Configure schedules, windows, thresholds, notifications"):
    - the SMTP host and port;
    - the TLS mode: STARTTLS or implicit TLS. A plaintext connection is refused;
    - how the relay authenticates the server: a username and password, or the server's IP address alone, allowed by the relay operator on their side (as Google Workspace's SMTP relay can). With IP authentication no username or password is stored, and the DEP-10 exception does not apply;
    - the sender address;
    - the operations-alert recipients: one or more email addresses for the operator role and for the deputy role (DEP-3). The addresses are configuration, never repository content.

    Saving is an audited command whose record names the fields changed, never the password. A **send test email** action reports success or a fixed safe error code. Email notifications (§13) use the same relay.
  - **The host copy.** A root-run job writes the settings the host alerts need to a root-owned mode-0600 file, at least hourly and after each change. Host alerts read only that file, so they still send when the application or database is down. If the job fails, the last good copy stays in use and the failure is itself alerted. The server is shared, so it uses the relay of one organization designated for server operations; in the first release that is the only organization.
  - **Alerts** go by email to the operator and deputy addresses, for the DEP-10 conditions: certificate expiry within 14 days, low disk space, a full backup or binary-log shipping run older than expected, the database refusing connections, and repeated 5xx responses; and also for a failed recurring job, including the host-copy job.
  - **Site unreachable** is checked from outside the server in two ways: a probe script, run on another host the agency controls, that sends through the same relay; and a hosted uptime monitor whose account and recipient list the owner keeps.
  - **Each alert** is sent once when its condition starts and once when it clears, and repeats at most every 6 hours while the condition lasts. Alert text holds no secrets and no personal data beyond the recipients' own addresses. Alert logs are short-lived and kept apart from audit records.

### 18.1 Required constraints [P]

1. `UNIQUE(organization_id, operation_id)` on `check_record`, `odometer_entry`, `deficiency` creation requests, and every other client-initiated create.
2. `check_obligation_link` unique per `(check_id, obligation_id)`; every link of one check is to an obligation of the same vehicle, item requirement and kit instance (SCH-5, SCH-6). Links recorded before amendment 6.0.0 keep the earlier rule: a second link carries a non-null `shared_rule_id`.
3. `obligation` unique on `(vehicle_id, item_requirement_id, kit_instance_id, due_at, requirement_version_id)` (before amendment 6.0.0, `item_id` in place of `item_requirement_id`), with a missing kit instance compared as a value (MariaDB `UNIQUE` treats NULLs as distinct, so the physical key must not rely on a nullable column alone).
4. A correction's author must equal the original's author (enforced in the command handler and by constraint where the engine allows).
5. An invalidation row requires a non-empty explanation and an administrator actor.
6. `deficiency_event` status transitions validated against §8.2.
7. Referential integrity everywhere; users are never hard-deleted.
8. `vehicle_item_requirement_version`: at any instant at most one requirement in force per `(vehicle, item, place)`, Not placed compared as a value; checked when a version is published, for its whole effective interval (CHK-11).

### 18.2 Migration to vehicle-owned checklists [C] (amendment 5.0.0)

At one recorded cutover instant, an all-or-nothing, restart-safe migration must:

1. Create a distinct vehicle-local position identity for every existing vehicle/legacy stable-slot pair and preserve the old-to-new mapping. Preserve labels, ordering, retired/sealable revisions and all references to their historical vehicle/position revision.
2. For each `(vehicle, legacy template)` pair, materialize exactly one independent vehicle-owned checklist, including immutable versions and resolved vehicle override contents. Preserve each vehicle's `template_id` identity ordering used by the current generator as the stable checklist-precedence ordinal used by CHK-10. Preserve every existing binding from that pair to its schedule slots, with the same start and stop instants, DST resolution and obligation-stop behavior. If one template served several slots, the one vehicle copy remains assigned to all those slots; if several templates were assigned to one slot, they remain distinct checklists assigned to that slot. Do not merge or duplicate per slot. Materialize all published future checklist-version and assignment boundaries in order so future due instants retain their prior resolved contents and timing. Preserve the Supervisor/Administrator assignment permissions and CFG-4 inactive/activation semantics.
3. Leave all old templates, overrides, bindings, position keys, checks, obligations, sessions, kit-list versions, cabinet seal events and audit events intact and immutable. Keep the old-to-new map available indefinitely and use it when any historical record is read after cutover. Preserve open sessions pinned to legacy checklist versions; preserve existing obligations and their due instants without recomputing; preserve kit-list versions and intact seal reliance because physical contents did not change. No old check, obligation, seal, session or audit event is reattributed or rewritten. Keep audit/source references so migration can be explained and safely replayed.
4. Validate before commit that all effective legacy checklist versions, schedule assignment intervals, position references, kit-list versions, seals, sessions and outstanding obligations are represented; refuse the whole migration without partial writes if any mapping is ambiguous or missing. Replaying the same migration operation is idempotent.

### 18.3 Migration to item requirements and session limits [C] (amendment 6.0.0)

1. For each vehicle, create one item requirement for each stock that a checklist inspects, with versions taken from the checklist versions in force or published for a later instant. Within one checklist, a later version that places the item elsewhere is a move: a new version of the same requirement, effective at that checklist version's instant. This holds only if no other checklist keeps that item in the old place over any part of the later version's effective interval. If another checklist does, the migration cannot tell a move from a split, so it refuses and lists the case. Different checklists placing the item in different places at the same instant create one requirement per place. Every other case the migration cannot decide is refused and listed the same way. Where two checklists place the same item in the same place with different requirements, refuse the whole migration without partial writes and list each conflict; a Supervisor first publishes matching versions. An item placed in different places by different checklists becomes one requirement per place.
2. Leave every checklist version, obligation, check, link, deficiency, suspension, verification scope, session and audit event intact and immutable. Keep a map indefinitely, and use it whenever an earlier record is read. Its key is the source row: `(vehicle, legacy checklist version, item row)`, and for a kit item also the kit-definition entry. Each key maps to exactly one item requirement and the requirement version covering that checklist version's effective interval. An earlier obligation, check, link, deficiency, suspension or verification-scope row is resolved through the checklist version and item row it pinned, never through its item and place alone. The same item and place in two checklist lineages, one moved and one not, therefore resolve to their own requirements. A deficiency whose place the map cannot decide is listed by the dry-run and blocks the migration until a Supervisor assigns its place; it is never attached to every place.
3. Give every existing session a captured resume limit of 6 h. Sessions whose end instant (SES-9) has passed read as ended from it, and the session job records their ending event at that instant. Several sessions of one checklist already open on a vehicle finish under SES-1, SES-8 and SES-9 without being ended early.
4. Checks recorded before the migration are not re-linked (SCH-2). The migration has a dry-run that writes nothing, validates before commit, and is idempotent on replay.

## 19. Derived-state rules (reproducibility)

**19.1 Effective check outcome.** For a correction chain: if an INVALIDATION event exists on any of its rows → the whole chain is *Invalidated* (excluded; no earlier outcome revives, INV-2a). Otherwise the latest correction by the same author is the effective outcome; earlier rows are *Superseded*.

**19.2 Obligation state.** Computed from the obligation, any determination (waived/reinstated), effective checks linked to it, active suspension, vehicle Out-of-Service exemption interval, and server time (§5.3–5.4).

**19.3 As-of.** The same functions, parameterized by `T`, over events with `server_recorded_at ≤ T` and rules with `effective_at ≤ T` (REG-6).

**19.4 Item attention state.** Red if any unresolved deficiency applies to the item (via scope) or the latest effective check is Failed/Unable; otherwise clear (§7.5). For an item inside a kit the state is kept per kit instance.

**19.5 Deficiency status.** Last valid lifecycle event; a recompute check is part of CI once a stack exists.

## 22. Glossary

- **Obligation** — a dated instance of a requirement that a check can satisfy.
- **Check** — one attributed result for one item.
- **Session** — an organizing container for checks; never itself evidence of compliance.
- **In progress** — a session that is Open and before its end instant (SES-9); at most one per vehicle checklist (SES-7).
- **Resume deadline** — the session's open instant plus the organization's resume limit captured at open (SES-8).
- **Item requirement** — one stock of an item on one vehicle, kept in one place at a time, with its quantity and other requirements. Moving it keeps the requirement. Every checklist on the vehicle that inspects it shares it (CHK-11).
- **Supersede** — a correction replaces the effective outcome of an earlier check by the same author; original kept.
- **Invalidate** — administrator exclusion of a check from compliance; original kept.
- **Suspension** — supervisor-defined pause of specific routine checks for a known defect; not a Pass.
- **Deficiency** — a reported defect that is tracked until it is verified fixed or otherwise closed. This specification, the code, the data and audit records use "deficiency"; everything people see calls it a **problem** (OQ-77, [issue #194](https://github.com/JustApu/CheckYourAmbulance/issues/194)).
- **Exempt** — obligation not generated because the vehicle is Out of Service.
- **Waived/Carried over** — obligation not required under a conditional rule.

## 24. Decision register

### 24.1 Classes

- **Blocking before coding (BBC)** — the data model, security or irreversible behavior of the affected area cannot be implemented safely without it.
- **Blocking before production (BBP)** — implementation may proceed against the safe default, but the feature must not go live until the owner decides or confirms.
- **Deferrable (DEF)** — a documented default is acceptable for the release shown; decision can wait.

"Owner approval" = the owner must explicitly approve the decision or default in writing on the PR/issue (§25).

## 25. Approval and amendment process

*(Resolves OQ-26 as a proposal; the owner must approve this section itself.)*

### 25.1 Lifecycle

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Draft** | Under review; no authority. |
| **Approved** | The owner has stated in writing, on the PR/issue, which version is approved and listing any decisions overridden. The `Status` line and version change to e.g. `1.0.0 — Approved` in the same commit. |
| **Amended** | A later approved change. |

An owner merge of the PR is **not by itself** approval of a Draft; approval must be explicit. Agents never approve or merge ([coordination procedures](docs/coordination.md#handoff-and-completion)).

### 25.2 Approval checklist (what must be true before the status flips)

1. Every **BBC** row in §24 has an owner-approved answer or an owner-approved safe default, recorded in the register with "Decision: …, date, approver".
2. Every **[OQ]** marker remaining in the body is either resolved or listed as BBP/DEF with an owner-approved interim.
3. Independent reviews were obtained from other agent families (auditability, authorization, concurrency, scheduling) and unresolved disagreements are recorded as `OWNER INPUT REQUIRED` items.
4. Link, consistency and traceability checks (§26) pass.

### 25.3 Follow-up changes upon approval

On approval (and not before), a single follow-up change updates [README.md](README.md), [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md), [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), [PROJECT_HANDOFF.md](PROJECT_HANDOFF.md), `REQUIREMENTS.md` and `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` so that: `SPEC.md` is named authoritative in exactly one place per topic; `REQUIREMENTS.md` and `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` are marked as the historical discovery record (with a banner pointing to the spec register) rather than competing instruction sources; and any registered linked-document blob IDs in `AGENTS.md` are refreshed in the same commit as per its rules.

### 25.4 Amendments

After approval, a change to product behavior or architecture is made by PR that: (a) states the reason and the affected requirement/AC IDs; (b) edits `SPEC.md` and bumps the version (**major**: removes or reverses an approved rule or invariant; **minor**: adds or materially changes behavior; **patch**: clarification with no behavior change); (c) appends to §27 Change log; (d) records any new unresolved decision in the §24 register with a class and safe default; (e) requires explicit owner approval for any minor/major change. Implementation issues must reference spec section/AC IDs. If code and spec diverge, the spec is not silently rewritten to match; either the implementation is fixed or an amendment is approved ([engineering standards](docs/engineering-standards.md#documentation-audit)).

### 25.5 Invariants requiring a major version

Changing any of the following is a major change: PRN-1–PRN-7, INV-3 (invalidation never closes a deficiency), COR-1 (author-only correction), OPS-2 (no automatic status change), the append-only history rule (AUD-1/AUD-3).

### A.3 Proposed recommendation **[P]**

1. **Do not choose A1** for production: ARC-2 (immutability against a compromised application) and ARC-7 (point-in-time recovery for as-of audits) are weak for a public-facing, audit-critical system.
2. **A3 is the working direction**: it honors the owner's language preference and database leaning. MySQL vs MariaDB is to be chosen in the spike by which one passes the verification tasks and has the better-maintained Perl driver and operations story on the chosen Linux distribution (U). A2 remains an alternative if the database tests fail; **B** is the fallback if ARC-6 or session/CSRF tooling proves inadequate in Perl.
3. The choice is hard to reverse, so make it through a short architecture issue that performs the tasks below with real installs rather than relying on this table. The decision is the owner's (OQ-24).

### A.4 Verification tasks for the architecture issue

1. Confirm from official documentation: framework support for WebSocket and/or server-sent events; maintained OIDC/Google ID-token validation (signature, issuer, audience, expiry, organization claim) and its security record; Perl driver maintenance for each database.
2. Prove ARC-2: a database account that cannot UPDATE or DELETE append-only tables, with a test that attempts both, and confirm the application connects only with that account.
3. Prove strict data handling: startup check that strict mode and constraint enforcement (unique, foreign key, CHECK) are active; tests that invalid or oversized values are rejected rather than coerced or truncated.
4. Prove CON-5 and REC-4: simultaneous check submissions for one `(vehicle,item)` and replayed operation ids, asserting one commit and correct conflict warnings under the database's actual isolation level.
5. Prove ARC-4/TIME-3: an idempotent obligation generator across spring-forward and fall-back boundaries, storing UTC.
6. Test backup and point-in-time restore to a chosen instant and rebuild projections (§19, AC-26).
7. Choose MySQL or MariaDB on those results and operations fit; review dependency footprint, patch cadence and public-exposure hardening (TLS termination, rate limiting, session settings).
8. Record the result as an OQ-24 decision for the owner to approve.
